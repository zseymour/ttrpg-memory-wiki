# Strong Start Bank

A bank of session-opening situations. The strong start is the scene that drops the PC into immediate action, choice, or pressure right after the recap. Never start passive ("you sit at the inn").

Browse this list when prepping. Adapt to your setting, system, and current campaign state. The pattern is: **a shot, a sound or sensation, a problem.** Then "what do you do?"

---

## In medias res action openers

### The ambush
You're on the road. The horses scream first — then you see the pit-trap at your feet, opening too late to stop. Three figures break from the treeline, blades drawn. What do you do?

### The chase already started
You're halfway down the alley before you register why you're running. A whistle pierces the fog behind you — closer than you'd like. The wet cobblestones give bad footing; ahead, the alley splits three ways. What do you do?

### Mid-fight cold open
The bandit's blade glances off your shoulder; pain blooms hot. Two more behind him, blocking the path. The merchant you swore to protect is on the ground, bleeding. What do you do?

### The fall
You're halfway down before you understand: the floor wasn't a floor, it was a trap. Stone walls rush past. The drop continues, longer than seems possible. What do you do?

### The explosion just happened
The blast threw you against the wall. Your ears ring. Through the smoke, the relic case is open and empty. Footsteps — running — leaving. What do you do?

## Decision-point openers

### The summons
The Duke's herald stands at your door. "Lord Cassivar requires your presence. Now. Tonight. Bring nothing but yourself." His tone is urgent. What do you do?

### The choice already made for you
You wake in a strange room — clean linens, sunlight, an unfamiliar smell. A figure dozes in the chair across from you. You can hear voices arguing in the next room — your name is mentioned. What do you do?

### The deadline
The letter is in your hand: "I will be at the harbor inn at sunset. Come alone. Bring the box." Sunset is in two hours. The box is in your pack, unopened. What do you do?

### The offer
The stranger across from you slides a heavy purse onto the table. "Half now. The other half when it's done. We need an answer tonight." What do you do?

### The threshold
You stand at the entrance. The brass knocker is shaped like a screaming face. From inside, you hear a single muffled scream — then silence. Your hand is on the door. What do you do?

## Sensory immersion openers

### The wrong morning
You wake. The light is wrong — too red, too low. Outside your window, the sky is the color of dried blood, and there are no birds. There should be birds. What do you do?

### The strange caller
You open your door at the hour-bell, expecting your usual courier. Instead, a child stands on your stoop — soaking wet, no umbrella, no parent. They say nothing. They hold out a folded letter sealed with familiar wax. What do you do?

### The smell
You smell it before you see it: copper and ozone, layered over something sweetly rotten. The corridor curves ahead. The smell is coming from around the bend. What do you do?

### The silence
You realize you haven't heard a sound in minutes. No wind, no insects, no distant calls. The forest holds its breath. Ten feet ahead, the path turns. What do you do?

### The wrongness
The shop you've been to a hundred times is different today. Same shelves, same goods, same shopkeeper smiling pleasantly behind the counter — but you don't recognize them, and they're greeting you like a regular. What do you do?

## NPC-driven openers

### The arrival
The door to the tavern slams open. A figure you haven't seen in fifteen years — bloodied, gasping, and impossibly here — collapses across your table. They look up at you and say one word: your name. What do you do?

### The confrontation
The captain's voice cuts across the courtyard: "Don't take another step." You stop. He's flanked by six soldiers; their crossbows are drawn. Something has happened that you don't yet understand. What do you do?

### The accusation
The marketplace freezes. The merchant's finger is leveled at you. His voice rises: "That's the one! That's the thief who took my daughter's ring!" People are staring. The Watch is pushing through the crowd. What do you do?

### The reunion
You almost walk past the figure leaning against the lamppost. Then you recognize the cloak — the one you gave them the last night you saw them. They look up. They've been waiting. What do you do?

### The plea
A woman is at your door, soaked from the rain. She presses something into your hand — wrapped in wet cloth — and whispers: "Don't open it here. Not until you're alone. Not unless you have to." Then she's gone before you can answer. What do you do?

## Mystery / investigation openers

### The body
You arrived at the Captain's request — but the Captain is on the floor, throat opened, eyes still surprised. The room is locked from inside. Your name is written in his blood beside him. What do you do?

### The disappearance
You wake from a fitful sleep to find the bedroll beside you cold and empty. The fire's down to coals. Your companion's pack is still here — but they are not. What do you do?

### The threatening gift
Wrapped in fine paper. Bound with crimson ribbon. On your bed when you returned to your room. There's no card. There's no sender. The package is the size of a small jewelry box. What do you do?

### The wrong corpse
The body in the casket is a stranger. The face on the funeral portrait is your old friend's — but the body is not. The mourners weep convincingly. What do you do?

### The map
The sailor's hand finds yours under the table; presses a folded square of waxed paper into your palm. He doesn't look at you. He says, loud enough for the room: "Never seen you before in my life." Then he leaves. What do you do?

## Combat / action openers

### The duel
You stand at the agreed clearing, blade drawn. Across from you, your opponent salutes — and immediately attacks, before your second has called the start. What do you do?

### The siege already underway
Arrows are sleeting overhead as you stagger up to the gatehouse. The captain hauls you behind the parapet. "About time. Tell me you brought reinforcements." Outside, the army numbers in the thousands. What do you do?

### The pursuit
You hit the rooftop running. Tiles slip under your feet. Behind, the Watch's whistles are joined by something you've never heard before — a higher, hunting cry. What do you do?

### The duel of words
The court goes silent. The Duke's voice is iron: "Repeat what you just said. In front of all of them. So we're certain." The room is full of his sworn vassals. What do you do?

## Quiet but pressured openers

### The waking
You wake in the dark. You're not in your bed. You're not anywhere familiar. Your hands are bound — but not tightly. You can hear someone breathing nearby. What do you do?

### The interview
The Inquisitor sits across from you in a plain wooden chair. She has a small cup of tea and an open ledger. "Now then," she says pleasantly, "let's begin again. Why don't you tell me — in your own words — where you were the night the bishop died?" What do you do?

### The proposal
The Lord lays the contract on the table. "It is a generous offer. My daughter is everything I have promised her to be. The benefits — to your house, to your family, to your future — are considerable." His eyes are cold. The contract is unsigned. What do you do?

### The reading of the will
The notary clears his throat. "To my son, I leave nothing but my regrets and the truth — the truth that I have hidden for thirty years, which he will find in the third drawer of my desk, in the false bottom, sealed with my mother's wax." Every face in the room turns to you. What do you do?

### The morning after
You wake in a fine bed. You don't remember the night. Across the room, the wedding garment is laid out — and a ring you don't recognize is on your finger. What do you do?

## Continuing campaign openers (mid-arc)

These work best when continuing from a known cliffhanger or active situation.

### Cliffhanger resumed
"You ended last session with the dagger raised, the cultist on his knees, and the moon a single hour from dark. The wind has shifted; the cult song has stopped. He's not pleading anymore. He's smiling. What do you do?"

### After downtime
"Two weeks have passed in [town/city]. Your wounds have healed; your debts haven't. This morning, the message arrived: [content]. What do you do?"

### After travel
"The road to [destination] took longer than expected. You arrive at dusk, road-weary and thinner than you started. The first thing you notice as you crest the rise is [unexpected thing]. What do you do?"

### Returning to a place
"You've been gone for [time]. The town looks different. [Specific change]. You stop at [familiar landmark] and find it [changed somehow]. What do you do?"

## Pattern: building your own

A strong start has these elements:

1. **A shot** — where is the camera? What does the PC see?
2. **A sound or sensation** — something the brain hears immediately.
3. **A problem** — the thing demanding action.
4. **A question** — "What do you do?"

Compose all four in three or four sentences. Cut everything that doesn't serve them.

**Bad opener:** "It's been a few days since the last session. You've been resting in the inn, recovering from the fight with the goblins. The innkeeper has been pleasant. You've eaten well. Today is sunny..."

**Better opener:** "It's three days later. You're in the common room, finishing breakfast, when the innkeeper sets a folded note in front of you. 'A man left this. Wouldn't say his name.' Outside, a crow lands on the windowsill, watching. What do you do?"

The pattern: cut the throat-clearing. Drop into the moment that matters.

# Encounters — Combat, Conflict, and Pressure

This reference covers how to design and run encounters, with particular attention to the duet context (one PC, no party safety net).

## The dramatic question

Every encounter answers a yes/no the encounter resolves:
- "Does the PC retrieve the relic before the cult finishes?"
- "Does the PC convince the duke to break his alliance?"
- "Does the PC escape the burning ship?"
- "Does the PC survive the goblin ambush?"

When the question is answered, **end the encounter.** Don't grind through HP after the outcome is decided. Narrate the cleanup.

## Beyond "kill all the bad guys"

Most encounters benefit from a non-combat win condition layered on top of (or instead of) combat. Pick one or more:

- **Retrieve** — grab the thing and get out. Time pressure.
- **Defend** — hold the position for N rounds.
- **Escort** — protect the VIP who can't fight.
- **Escape** — exit is the goal, not victory.
- **Race** — get there first.
- **Capture alive** — non-lethal restraint is harder.
- **Disable** — stop the ritual / shut down the device. Clock-driven.
- **Demoralize / rout** — break the enemy's will, not their bones.
- **Manipulate terrain** — collapse the bridge, flood the chamber, spike the cannon.

Layered objectives convert grind into drama. "Defeat the cultists" is a slog. "Defeat the cultists *while preventing the ritual from completing* (clock fills in 4 rounds)" is a story.

## Encounter checklist

Before running any encounter, answer:

1. **What is the dramatic question?** (What does winning mean?)
2. **What is the fail state?** (What does losing mean — and is it interesting?)
3. **Is there a clock or threshold?** (Do they have N rounds? Until something specific?)
4. **What's telegraphed?** (Did the PC have warning?)
5. **What's the terrain?** (At least 2–3 interactable features.)
6. **What's the morale break point?** (When do enemies flee, surrender, retreat?)
7. **What's the escape route?** (For PC and for fleeing enemies — see "Recurring villain escape" below.)

## Telegraphing — the contract that earns you the right to hurt the PC

A player who walks into a buzzsaw with no warning has been wronged. A player who pushes past three signposts has consented.

### The Three Signposts rule
Before any major threat lands, the PC encounters at least three escalating warnings:

1. **Distant signpost** — rumor, lore, faded mural, tavern gossip, history book, scholarly reference.
2. **Environmental signpost** — burned village, dead adventurers, claw marks, sulfur smell, scorched earth.
3. **Immediate signpost** — vibration in the floor, low growl, temperature spike, shadow moving wrong.

A PC who pushes past all three has made an informed choice. The fairness test is satisfied.

### Telegraph monster abilities before they fire
Don't surprise-launch a special attack. Set it up.

- Dragon's breath: bones in ash near the lair; charred walls; the dragon inhales deeply (one round before).
- Ogre rage charge: ogre flexes, head swelling red; bellows; charges next round.
- Bulette burrow: rumble; tremor in the ground; bulette breaks surface next round.
- Spell ritual: caster begins gestures; visible energy gathers; spell fires next round.

The "round before" rule gives the PC one round to react. In duet especially — where one bad surprise round can end the campaign — telegraphing is non-negotiable.

### Fairness test
After any bad outcome: could the player point to the moment they could have made a different choice with the information they had?
- **Yes** → fair. Move on.
- **No** → unfair. Adjust outcome (use Inspiration / hero point / re-narrate as setback rather than disaster), and telegraph more next time.

## Theater of the mind vs. grid

Choose based on system and context, not personal preference:

| Context | Use |
|---|---|
| Tactical-heavy systems (PF2e, GURPS, 4e, OSE w/ minis) | Grid (or zone abstraction) |
| Narrative systems (PbtA, Blades, Fate) | Theater of the mind |
| 5e | Either; default to TotM, switch to grid for big set-pieces |
| OSR | Theater of the mind w/ player-mapped grid |
| One-on-one duet | Theater of the mind almost always |

### Theater of the mind procedure (zone-based)
- Define zones by **evocative name**, not feet: "the crumbling bridge," "the blood-altar dais," "the choke point at the door."
- Default distance: ~25 feet between adjacent zones if asked.
- Most fights are 1 zone; medium fights 2–3; large fights 3–5.
- A turn = act + move within zone, OR move one zone + act.
- **Recap positions at the start of each PC turn.** "You're at the dais. Three cultists are at the altar across the chamber, blocking the exit. The brazier is between you."
- When the player asks "is there X?" (cover, a chandelier, a window), answer fast. Default to yes if it fits the location's flavor — collaborative fiction is the point.

### Grid procedure
- Convert key features to a sketch or shared image.
- Track movement in feet/squares.
- Use the system's positioning rules (5e: opportunity attacks, cover; PF2e: flanking, reach, action economy).
- Slower but tactically richer. Reserve for fights where positioning matters.

## Action-oriented monsters (for duet bosses)

The default 5e stat block underperforms for solo or small-group play because it's designed against a 4-PC party. For a duet boss, juice it:

1. **Double or triple HP.** Don't raise AC — the player needs hits to land.
2. **Add at least one movement action that doesn't provoke.** Bosses shouldn't get trapped in melee.
3. **Add 2+ reactions** to common PC behaviors.
4. **Add 3 villain actions, one per round, on initiative count 20:**
   - **Round 1:** setup or summon (call minions, raise barrier, position).
   - **Round 2:** battlefield-shaping (collapse a wall, create difficult terrain, bestow a curse).
   - **Round 3:** ultimate (the desperation move — the move the boss saves for when they're losing).
5. **Optional: legendary resistance** (1–3/day) so a single spell doesn't end the boss.

This converts a 1-round HP-grind into a 3–5 round dramatic fight with shifting stakes.

## Minion rules (for duet "epic" feel)

Throw a swarm of one-hit-kill minions at a PC for the cinematic "I cleave through them" feel without TPK risk.

- **Any damage that hits = minion drops.** No HP tracking.
- **Group attacks for damage.** A group of N minions deals damage = N × base. Or roll once and apply to all in melee range.
- **Initiative as a group.** Don't run them individually.
- Use 4–8 minions per duet PC for satisfying carnage.

This is the fix for the duet "I want to feel powerful but also threatened" tension.

## Encounter math by system

### D&D 5e
- The XP-budget math (CR / Encounter Multiplier) is unreliable past tier 1.
- For duet (1 PC):
  - **Easy:** 1 enemy of CR ≤ ¼ PC level
  - **Medium:** 1 enemy of CR ≤ ½ PC level
  - **Hard:** 1 enemy of CR ≈ PC level
  - **Deadly:** 1 enemy of CR > PC level (use action-oriented adjustments)
  - **Group:** 2–4 enemies of CR ≤ 1/8 PC level (or use minion rules)
- Never more than 4 active enemies at once. Use waves.
- A single CR-equal monster against a solo PC is more dangerous than CR suggests because there's no party to spread damage. Adjust HP/damage downward.

### Pathfinder 2e
- XP budget by relative level: −4 = 10 XP, −3 = 15, −2 = 20, −1 = 30, level = 40, +1 = 60, +2 = 80, +3 = 120, +4 = 160.
- Threat budgets: Trivial 40, Low 60, Moderate 80, Severe 120, Extreme 160.
- For duet, divide budget by 4 (since the budgets assume 4 PCs).

### Call of Cthulhu
- Combat is rare and lethal. Most monsters are encounter-ending without proper preparation.
- Use sanity loss as the main threat axis, not HP.
- Most "encounters" are investigative tension, not combat.

### Blades / FitD
- No CR; threat is fictional position. A single thug in Risky position with Standard effect is a real threat.
- Clocks drive encounter pacing more than HP.

### PbtA
- No encounter math. Threats have **moves** that fire when the GM has license. Difficulty is a function of the move's hardness and the PC's stats.

### OSR
- Encounter difficulty is *deliberately uncalibrated* — the world contains threats the PC cannot defeat. Player skill is to assess, retreat, and outmaneuver.
- Telegraph clearly so the player can choose to engage or avoid.

### Year Zero
- Combat uses the same dice pool. Push for re-rolls but pay the thematic cost.
- Most YZE games include a Wound / Critical Injury table — use it; injuries persist.

## Monster tactics by intelligence

Use the stat block's mental scores to drive behavior. (This adapts Keith Ammann's framework.)

| Int | Behavior |
|---|---|
| ≤4 | Animal: instinctive, one preferred tactic, no adaptation. Attacks nearest, flees on injury. |
| 5–7 | Bestial-cunning: simple tactic, slight adaptation. Will gang up, use terrain crudely. |
| 8–11 | Common: unsophisticated planning. Uses terrain, basic teamwork. |
| 12–13 | Smart: plans, coordinates, switches tactics mid-fight. |
| 14+ | Tactical: targets enemy weaknesses (kills the wizard first), exploits openings. |
| 18+ | Genius: predicts PC behavior, sets traps, may have layered contingencies. |

| Wis | Behavior |
|---|---|
| ≤4 | No fear; fights to the death always. |
| 5–9 | Fights past reason; flees only when broken. |
| 10–13 | Knows when to flee; will retreat at half HP if losing. |
| 14+ | Reads the situation. Surrenders, parleys, retreats strategically. |

**Most evolved creatures flee around 30% HP** unless ideology overrides (zealots, cornered animals, undead, oath-bound).

Apply morale rolls (2d6 vs. morale rating, default 7–9): at first casualty, half strength, leader fall.

## Recurring villain escape

A villain who dies the first time the PC engages is wasted. Set up escape routes in advance; show the cost.

### Five escape methods (use in rotation):

1. **Telepresence appearance.** The first encounter is a sending, scry, illusion, or projection. The PC can't hurt the villain at all. The villain monologues, then disappears.

2. **Pre-set escape route.** Teleport circle, trapdoor, summoned exit, dimension door. Telegraph it ("you see runes on the floor; they're glowing") so the PC could potentially disrupt with quick action.

3. **Decoy / projected image.** What the PC kills was an illusion or simulacrum. The real villain watches from elsewhere. Reveal this only if they "kill" the villain.

4. **Minion buffer.** 3–4 lackeys who delay the PC. Villain monologues, exits behind the minion line. PC must defeat minions first; villain has escaped by then.

5. **Resource-cost escape.** Villain consumes a one-shot artifact (potion of teleport, scroll, charge of a magic item). Show the cost — left behind: shattered artifact, blood trail, scarred face, broken wand.

**Crucial:** every escape costs the villain something visible. Bleeding scar, lost minion, broken artifact, exposed plot. Even unwon fights show the villain *losing something*.

**Disclose at session zero:** "Recurring villains may escape." Avoids the player feeling cheated.

## Villain monologues

Under 30 seconds. Reveal one new fact (lore, motivation, plan, or personal connection). Land a personal jab tied to PC backstory. End on an action — never on the speech.

**Bad:** "You will never defeat me. I am the dark lord. I have ruled these lands for a thousand years and I will continue to rule for ten thousand more. Your puny resistance amuses me. I crush nations beneath my heel and I will crush you next." [Endless. Generic. Player tunes out.]

**Better:** "I knew your father. He begged. *[turns]* Kill her." [One new fact. Personal jab. Ends on action.]

## Combat narration patterns

Don't say "you hit, 12 damage." Wrap it.

**Round 1 (full description):**
"You jam your blade between his ribs. The orc grunts, blood frothing at his lips. *That's 14 damage; he's bloodied.* He swings the axe back overhead — *roll Dex.*"

**Round 3 (compressed; still narrative):**
"He blocks, ripostes — *17, hits, 8 damage.* You stagger back into the brazier."

**Round 5 (terse, keep tempo):**
"Trade — both hit. You: 6 from his axe. Him: 9 from yours; he's reeling."

Rotate verbs: lunge, slash, hack, sweep, parry, drive, push, twist, slam, batter, ram.
Rotate body parts: shoulder, ribs, gut, thigh, jaw, knee, hand, neck.
Rotate environmental: brazier, table, broken glass, hanging chain, smoking lamp.

Cut description short when the fight is mostly resolved. Long fights cannot sustain full cinematic density.

## Ending encounters

End decisively. Don't drag.

- **Last enemy at 1 HP:** narrate the killing blow without rolling.
- **Boss broken:** the boss surrenders, escapes, or dies in description.
- **Player overwhelms:** narrate the rest as cleanup. "You finish them in moments."
- **Player loses:** see "Failure menu" in `adjudication.md`. Narrate the consequence — capture, retreat, scarring, lost objective — not gratuitous death (unless death is what the player is signed up for).

After the encounter:
- Update `pc.md` (HP, resources, conditions).
- Update `npcs.md` (NPC status — alive/dead/missing).
- Update `factions.md` if a faction was affected.
- Note any new clues or revelations.
- Pause for breath. Combat is intense; let the player exhale.

# Blades in the Dark / Forged in the Dark — System-Specific Guide

Covers Blades in the Dark, Scum and Villainy, Band of Blades, Wicked Ones, A Nocturne, and the broader FitD family. The architecture is consistent.

## Core mechanic

**Roll a dice pool of d6s.** Look at the highest die:
- **6:** full success.
- **4-5:** partial success / cost.
- **1-3:** miss / consequence.
- **Multiple 6s (critical):** improved success / extra effect / position improvement.

**Pool size = action rating** (0–4 dots) plus modifiers from gear, allies, scene factors. Minimum 1 die; pool of 0 rolls 2d6 and takes lowest.

The GM does not roll. The fiction sets stakes; the player rolls; the GM narrates.

## Position and Effect

The two big GM levers:

### Position (how bad it goes if it goes bad)

- **Controlled:** dominant advantage. Worst case = hesitation, minor lost ground.
- **Risky** (default): head-to-head. Worst case = harm, complications, asset loss.
- **Desperate:** overreach, beyond capability. Worst case = severe harm, dire complications.

### Effect (how much you accomplish on success)

- **Limited:** partial; less than fully effective.
- **Standard:** what you'd expect for the action.
- **Great:** increased impact; bonus benefit.

The GM sets both before the roll. They're shaped by:
- The PC's position and tools.
- The opposition.
- The scale of what's being attempted.
- Established fiction.

### Trade Position for Effect

Key GM lever: offer to **trade Position for Effect.** "You can do this Risky for Standard effect, or Desperate for Great effect — your call."

This is the system's pressure valve. Players willingly take Desperate rolls when the stakes are right.

### Telegraphing position

**Always tell the player the position and effect before they roll.** "This is Risky for Standard effect — a guard around the corner could see you, but you're confident about the lock."

The transparency is the whole point. Players make informed choices.

## Devil's Bargains

Before the roll, the GM (or any other player) may offer a **Devil's Bargain:** an additional complication that happens **regardless of the roll's outcome.** Player accepts for **+1d.**

Examples:
- Collateral damage to bystanders.
- A faction's clock advances.
- Betrayal — a contact gets sour on the PC.
- The PC suffers harm.
- An item is lost or broken.
- A new enemy is created.

**Player has final say on accepting.** GM has final say on the bargain's validity (no joke bargains).

Devil's Bargains should:
- Reveal character (force a thematic choice).
- Surface offscreen threats (make factions matter).
- Add narrative texture (not just punish mechanically).

## Resistance Rolls (the system's failure-into-fiction engine)

After the GM announces a consequence, the player can **resist** it:

1. Player declares resist.
2. Player rolls a resistance roll using a relevant attribute (Insight, Prowess, Resolve).
3. **Stress cost = 6 minus highest die.**
4. The consequence is reduced or avoided.

Examples:
- Consequence: "Take 2 harm from the explosion."
- Resist with Prowess: roll 3d6, get 5. Stress cost = 1. Reduce to 1 harm.

**Critical (multiple 6s):** clear 1 stress instead of taking it.
**No 6, no 5, no 4:** take 5 stress.

This is the player's safety net. **Be liberal with offering resistance opportunities** — it's how the system manages danger.

## Stress and Trauma

PCs have **9 stress boxes.** When stress maxes out:
- Take a trauma (mark a behavior: Cold, Haunted, Obsessed, Paranoid, Reckless, Soft, Unstable, Vicious).
- Stress resets to 0.
- 4 traumas = retire the PC.

Stress refills during downtime via **vice indulgence.**

**In duet:** stress and trauma are character development. Lean into the trauma names; they become roleplay anchors.

## Progress Clocks

Circle in 4, 6, or 8 segments. Tick when:
- Action succeeds (advance progress).
- Action complications (advance opposing clock).
- Time passes / events occur.
- Fortune roll determines the result.

### Clock types:
- **Linked clocks:** one fills, another starts.
- **Racing clocks:** PC vs. enemy. Whoever fills first wins.
- **Faction clocks:** factions advance during downtime.
- **Countdown clocks:** segments tick *down* to a hard event.

**Show clocks openly.** They're the system's tension manager. Hidden clocks defeat the purpose.

## Score / Mission structure

Blades organizes play into **Scores** (heists / missions). Structure:

1. **Choose the score.** What's the job? Who's the target?
2. **Engagement Roll.** Single roll determines the **starting position** (Controlled / Risky / Desperate). Skip planning entirely.
3. **Cut to the action.** Start in the middle.
4. **Resolve via flashbacks** for any prep that should have happened.
5. **End at success / failure / withdrawal.**

### The engagement roll skip

PbtA / FitD's elegant solution to "we waste an hour planning the heist." Don't plan. Cut in. Use flashbacks to retroactively establish prep.

## Flashbacks

Player declares a flashback to retroactively show prep. **GM sets stress cost:**
- 0 stress: ordinary, expected prep.
- 1 stress: complex or unlikely prep.
- 2+ stress: elaborate, expensive, or implausible prep.

Resolve as a normal action roll. The flashback cannot undo what's now established.

**Best moment for flashbacks:** right after a complication. ("You got caught at the gate — did you have a plan for this?" "Yes — flash back to me bribing the night watchman last week. ... Stress 1, roll Sway.")

In any non-FitD system: charge a token (Inspiration, Hit Die, Fate Point) per flashback. Limit ~1–2 per session.

## Downtime

Between scores, PCs do downtime activities. Standard = 2 actions per PC.

Activity options:
- **Acquire Asset** (gain a tool / contact / leverage).
- **Indulge Vice** (clear stress; risk Overindulgence).
- **Long Term Project** (advance a multi-step goal; tick a project clock).
- **Recover** (heal harm; with help from a friendly contact).
- **Reduce Heat** (manage faction reputation).
- **Train** (improve playbook XP).

Faction clocks advance during downtime. **The world acts when the PCs aren't on screen.**

## Harm

Three levels of harm: Lesser, Moderate, Severe. Each is a *named injury* that mechanically affects play (e.g., "Twisted Ankle" = -1d to relevant rolls).

Heal in downtime via Recover action.

**Death** in Blades is rare and earned. PCs at Severe harm + another Severe = death. Most failures result in capture, escape, or maiming, not death.

In duet, lean into named harm. It's character-shaping.

## Factions

Blades is a faction-rich game. Each district has factions; PCs have standing with each (-3 to +3).

- **+3:** allied, friendly. Provides aid.
- **0:** neutral.
- **-3:** war. Enemies act against PCs.

PC actions shift faction standing. Reputation accrues from successful scores.

**Faction clocks** track each faction's long-term goals. Advance during downtime based on faction logic.

## Running FitD in duet

Blades is well-suited to duet (and even runs as solo / co-op in other FitD games like Ironsworn-derivatives). Adjustments:

- **One PC means one playbook.** Reduce action ratings slightly to compensate for narrower coverage, or use a sidekick / cohort.
- **The PC's crew can be a single contact + the PC.** Or a small cell of 2-3.
- **Flashbacks are extra useful.** Lean into them.
- **Devil's Bargains create texture** without requiring a party to absorb consequences.
- **Faction politics scale beautifully** to one PC navigating intrigue.

## What FitD does that other systems don't

- **Stress as a renewable resource** that converts danger into character development.
- **Position/Effect** as the GM's primary lever (not DCs).
- **Engagement Roll + Flashbacks** as a planning skip.
- **Explicit faction clocks** as worldbuilding mechanics.
- **Score-based session structure** (mission → downtime → mission).

## Common FitD mistakes

- **Over-narrating instead of letting position/effect do the work.** State position and effect; let the player decide.
- **Refusing Devil's Bargains.** Offer them often.
- **Hiding clocks.** Show them. The tension comes from visibility.
- **Skipping resistance offers.** When a consequence is harsh, the player can resist. Tell them so.
- **Pre-planning scores.** The system is designed to skip planning. Trust it.
- **Not advancing factions during downtime.** The world doesn't pause.

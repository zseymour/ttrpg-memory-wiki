# Burning Wheel — System-Specific Guide

Burning Wheel is a deeply character-driven system where **Beliefs, Instincts, and Traits (BITs)** are the engine of play. The GM's central job is to challenge those Beliefs.

## Resolution

**Core mechanic:** roll a dice pool ≥ Obstacle (Ob). Each die that hits the **shade threshold** is a success.

- **Black shade** (default): success on 4-6. Most starting characters.
- **Grey shade:** success on 3-6. Heroic.
- **White shade:** success on 2-6. Legendary.

**Need successes ≥ Ob.** If the pool meets or exceeds the Ob in successes, the test succeeds.

**Open-ended sixes:** when you roll a 6, that die succeeds AND you reroll it. Cascading sixes can produce surprising successes against high obstacles.

## Obstacles (Ob)

- **Ob 1:** Routine.
- **Ob 2:** Difficult.
- **Ob 3:** Hard.
- **Ob 4:** Tough.
- **Ob 5:** Very Tough.
- **Ob 6:** Heroic.
- **Ob 7-10:** Legendary, near-impossible.

Default to Ob 2-3 for most rolls.

**Setting Ob:** based on situation. Use the system's task lists when available; eyeball when not. The Ob should be a real challenge for the character's pool size.

## Beliefs, Instincts, Traits (BITs)

The character's mechanical-narrative engine. **Every PC has 3 Beliefs, 3 Instincts, and Traits.**

### Beliefs (the engine)

A Belief is an ethical or goal statement WITH a plan of action.

- **Bad:** "I value family." (Vague.)
- **Good:** "My brother's killer must die — I will hunt him through the lawless lands until I find him."

Beliefs are:
- **Specific.**
- **Actionable.**
- **Meant to be challenged.**

The GM's central job: **challenge Beliefs every session.** Construct situations that put pressure on at least one Belief. Force the player to act on their Belief, modify it, or betray it.

### Instincts (the reflexes)

"Always X" / "Never Y" / "When X, Y."

- "Always check for traps."
- "Never trust a Sorcerer."
- "When threatened, draw my sword."

Instincts trigger automatically — no roll needed. Use them as fictional anchors and as moments of "your instinct kicks in."

### Traits (the texture)

Three types:
- **Character traits:** color and flavor. "Red-haired," "Hot-blooded."
- **Call-on traits:** 1/session, reroll a relevant test. "Cool-headed in a crisis" — call on for a Steel test.
- **Die traits:** modify rolls. "Tough" might add 1D to Steel.

## Artha (the reward economy)

Three currencies for engaging BITs:

- **Fate Points:** used to enable open-ended rolls on a single die, or call on a trait. Awarded for Belief enactment.
- **Persona Points:** used for +1D on a roll, double-tap a wound, declare a connection. Awarded for embodying a Belief or hitting Instinct.
- **Deeds Points:** used for +2D on a roll, redeem yourself from defeat. Awarded for major heroic moments tied to Beliefs.

**Award generously.** Artha-starved play is BW miserable. End of every session, ask:
- Did the PC act on a Belief? → Fate.
- Did the PC act despite cost (or against a Belief in a Belief-challenging way)? → Persona.
- Did the PC achieve something legendary tied to a Belief? → Deeds.

Award visibly. Players need to see the connection between BIT engagement and reward.

## "Let It Ride"

One roll resolves the whole intent for the situation. **The GM cannot demand a redo until circumstances genuinely change.**

- PC rolls Stealth to sneak through the camp. Result holds for the entire camp. No re-rolls per guard.
- PC rolls Persuasion to convince the duke. Result holds; the GM can't demand another roll later in the same conversation.
- New circumstance (different camp; different topic) = new roll.

**Failed roll doesn't mean "no."** Twist the intent — door opens but rival arrives; PC sneaks past but is followed; duke is convinced but at a price.

This is the BW failure-into-fiction principle. Failures advance the plot.

## Failure economy

In BW, failure is generative. **The GM owes a complication or twist on failure**, never just "you don't succeed."

- The lock is opened, but the noise alerts the watchman.
- The PC convinces the noble, but at the cost of swearing service.
- The PC reaches the cliff top, but their gear was lost in the climb.

This is where the system's narrative power lives.

## Combat (the "Range and Cover," "Fight!," "Bloody Versus" subsystems)

BW combat is system-heavy. Three resolution methods:

### Bloody Versus (fast, scripted)
Each combatant scripts 1 action per volley. Compare results; apply damage. Single-volley resolution.

### Range and Cover (positioning)
For ranged combat or stalking. Scripted positioning + actions. Resolves in 1-3 volleys.

### Fight! (full tactical combat)
Scripted action by action. Each combatant writes 3 actions per volley; reveal simultaneously. Heavy mechanics; for major fights only.

**Use sparingly.** Most combat in BW is brief and decisive. Reserve Fight! for climactic scenes.

### Damage and wounds

**Mortal Wound** = the threshold past which the character dies. Calculated from PT × FOR.

Wounds cause **wound penalties** — dice subtracted from rolls. A character can die from accumulated wounds even if no single hit is mortal.

Healing is slow. Wounds matter.

## Steel (the courage stat)

When facing horror, surprise, dragons, etc., the PC tests **Steel**:
- **Pass:** maintain action; possibly Hesitation 1.
- **Fail:** Hesitation increases (1-6). High Hesitation = extended freeze, longer recovery.

Use Steel tests for genuine moments of crisis — don't trivialize.

## Circles (social network)

Circles is the skill for finding people. Test Circles (with Ob set by how specific / important / friendly the person is) to find or summon someone.

- **Pass:** find them, can deal with them.
- **Fail:** find someone who's actively against the PC's interest. The GM names this NPC.

A wonderful generative tool. Use Circles to introduce new NPCs.

## Resources (wealth)

Resource tests instead of tracking GP. Roll vs. Ob (set by item cost / time / influence required). Failed Resources tests lower the character's Resources stat ("taxed").

Useful for abstracting wealth and gear management.

## Duel of Wits

Social combat. Like Fight!, but for arguments. Each side scripts arguments; resolve simultaneously; track Body of Argument (HP-equivalent).

Resolves any major social conflict mechanically.

## Running BW in duet

BW is well-suited to duet because the system is so character-focused. Adjustments:

- **PC's Beliefs anchor every session.** Always have at least one situation pressing on a Belief.
- **Artha awards are visible and frequent.** The PC is the only one — give them clear feedback.
- **NPCs get Beliefs too.** Give every recurring NPC at least one Belief; their actions follow it.
- **Use Circles liberally.** Generates NPCs naturally.
- **Combat is rare and brutal.** Don't run Fight! every session. Bloody Versus for most clashes.

## What BW does that other systems don't

- **BITs as the campaign engine.** No other system makes character-belief central this way.
- **Artha as visible reward for engagement.** Mechanically reinforces narrative play.
- **Let It Ride.** Forces consequences to matter.
- **Failure as generative.** Twists, not just refusals.
- **Open-ended sixes.** Drama in the math.

## Common BW mistakes

- **Vague Beliefs.** "I want to be good" is not playable. Demand specificity at session zero.
- **Letting Beliefs sit untouched.** Challenge every session.
- **Not awarding Artha.** Players feel unrewarded; engagement drops.
- **Refusing failures.** "You didn't pick the lock; nothing happens." Twist instead.
- **Calling for too many rolls.** Many actions resolve fictionally.
- **Running Fight! for every brawl.** Use Bloody Versus for most combats.

## BW session zero specifics

Beyond the standard session zero:

- **Build PC together** (lifepaths, then BITs). The lifepath system is half the fun.
- **Establish 3 Beliefs** that point at *something concrete* the campaign will pressure.
- **3 Instincts** that should fire often.
- **Confirm: am I willing to challenge these Beliefs?** Both players answer yes.

The Beliefs **are** the campaign's spine. If they're weak, the campaign is weak.

## A note on the lighter variant: Mouse Guard / Torchbearer

Burning Wheel's progeny include lighter games:

- **Mouse Guard:** simplified BW, GM-Player turn structure, Patrol-Leader duties. Excellent for duet.
- **Torchbearer:** dungeon-crawler version, attrition-heavy, resource management.

Both share BW's BIT-driven architecture but with lighter mechanical load. Recommended starting points if BW feels heavy.

# Call of Cthulhu 7e — System-Specific Guide

CoC is a horror investigation system. Combat is rare and lethal. Most encounters are about information, sanity, and mounting dread.

## Resolution

**Core mechanic:** roll d100 vs. skill value. Roll under = success.

**Difficulty levels:**
- **Regular:** roll under skill value.
- **Hard:** roll under ½ skill value.
- **Extreme:** roll under ⅕ skill value.
- **Critical:** roll 01.
- **Fumble:** roll 96-100 (if skill < 50%) or 100 (if skill ≥ 50%).

**Setting difficulty:** the Keeper sets it based on conditions. Default Regular for unopposed; Hard or Extreme for difficult or opposed circumstances.

## Bonus and penalty dice

Replace fiddly modifiers. Roll the tens d10 once normally, plus 1–2 additional tens d10s.

- **Bonus dice:** keep the lowest tens.
- **Penalty dice:** keep the highest tens.

Combine ones d10 with whichever tens you keep. Apply for advantage, disadvantage, environmental factors.

## Pushed rolls

After failure, the investigator may **push** the roll — re-attempt, justifying renewed effort in fiction. **Failure on a pushed roll is severe and thematic** — sanity loss, encountering monsters, equipment loss, etc.

Examples:
- Library Use (failed): the librarian asks you to leave. Push: she calls the police, OR you find the book but it draws attention from the wrong people.
- Climb (failed): you can't make it. Push: you fall, take damage, and the rope/handhold breaks.

Pushing is a key dramatic lever. **Encourage pushing** for tension; deliver real consequences when it fails.

## Sanity (the core mechanic)

Investigators have **starting Sanity = POW × 5** (POW is a stat). Sanity can be lost through:
- Witnessing horrors (small loss: 0/1d3, 1/1d6, etc.)
- Mythos creatures (large loss: 1/1d6+, 1d4/1d10, etc.)
- Reading Mythos tomes (per tome, per chapter)

**Format X/Y:** lose X if you make the Sanity roll, Y if you fail. Roll 1d100 vs. current Sanity.

### Sanity loss thresholds:
- **Lose 5+ in one moment:** trigger a **Bout of Madness**. Roll on table:
  - **Real-time** (1d10 rounds of in-fiction reaction): faint, flee, paranoia, hallucinate, etc.
  - **Summary** (1d10 hours/days time-skip): wake elsewhere with consequences.
- **Sanity below ⅕ max:** Indefinite Insanity. Lasts indefinitely; recovery requires extended therapy / closure / removal of stressor.
- **Sanity at 0:** permanently insane. Investigator retires.

**Recovery:** time, therapy, dealing with the source. Hard.

### Running sanity well

- **Don't stack sanity hits in a single scene.** Two big losses + several small losses in one session is enough.
- **Telegraph horror.** Don't ambush with cosmic dread. Build to it.
- **Make sanity loss narrative.** Describe what the investigator perceives. Let them describe their reaction.
- **Bouts of madness should be playable, not just punitive.** Give the player agency in describing them.

## Combat (avoid when possible)

CoC combat is dangerous and often non-survivable. Most monsters can kill a normal investigator in 1-2 hits.

**Initiative:** highest DEX acts first. Tied DEX = simultaneous.

**Attack:** roll under Fighting (Brawl/Sword/Firearms/etc.) skill. Defender can:
- **Dodge:** roll under Dodge skill; success negates.
- **Fight back:** roll under their own Fighting skill; if they roll *better* (higher level of success), they hit instead.

**Damage:** weapon's damage dice + DB (Damage Bonus, from STR+SIZ table). Subtract from HP.

**Major Wound:** half max HP or more from one source = Major Wound. CON roll or fall unconscious; if fail, dying.

**Dying:** unconscious; First Aid before next round, or take CON roll each round to stabilize. Fail three rounds = dead.

**Combat rare and brief:** most CoC sessions involve 0 or 1 combat. Investigation and mounting dread carry sessions.

## Investigation procedures (the heart of CoC)

CoC is built for mystery. Apply these specifically:

### Idea Roll (the key safety mechanism)

When investigators are stuck, the Keeper prompts: "Make an Idea Roll." Roll d100 vs. INT × 5.

- **Success:** Keeper provides a hint, lead, or relevant detail.
- **Failure:** they're really stuck. Drop a proactive clue (an NPC arrives, a body shows up, etc.).

This is the explicit failsafe. Use it. Don't let investigations stall.

### Spot Hidden, Library Use, Listen, Other Lore

These are the workhorse investigation skills. Apply them generously. Critical clues bypass rolls entirely (Gumshoe principle — even though CoC isn't Gumshoe, the meta-rule applies).

**Roll for bonus information,** not for whether the clue exists.

### Cthulhu Mythos skill

A unique skill that *only goes up* through exposure to Mythos horror. **Higher Cthulhu Mythos = lower max Sanity.** Knowledge corrupts. Use this thematically.

## Pulling Punches

The Keeper isn't bound to kill on every bad roll if the investigators have made earnest effort. **Lethality must remain present** — but a Major Wound that would kill can sometimes leave the investigator badly injured / hospitalized / scarred instead.

Caveat: don't pull punches so often the player loses fear of consequence. Use selectively, when the dice produce a result that would end the campaign rather than the scene.

## Luck

Investigators have a **Luck** stat (rolled like other stats). Spend Luck to:
- **Improve a roll** by adding to/subtracting from the d100 result (1 Luck point = 1 point shift).
- **Push for survival** — though rules vary; the principle is "spend luck to skirt disaster."

Luck does not regenerate during play (only between sessions/adventures). Use it sparingly; it's the player's safety net.

## Investigator development

After scenarios:
- **Skill checks marked during play** can be rolled for improvement. Roll d100 over current skill = +1d10 improvement.
- **Sanity recovery** based on scenario success.
- **Backstory connections, mementos, ideologies** are mechanical anchors. Engage them.

## Running a CoC duet

CoC is well-suited to duet because:
- The horror is intimate.
- Investigation rewards close attention.
- Sanity arc is character-defining.
- One investigator's descent is a complete story.

Adjustments:
- **Less combat-lethal.** A solo investigator dying ends the campaign. Provide warnings, escape routes, and chances to flee.
- **More NPC depth.** The investigator's allies, contacts, family — these are the social fabric that provides anchors before they fray.
- **Use the Pelgrane One-2-One pattern:** treat NPCs as **Sources** (key contacts who provide leads) and **Edges** (specific contacts the player can leverage). Lean into named, recurring contacts as the investigator's lifeline.
- **Sanity arc IS the campaign.** A duet CoC campaign is often the story of one investigator's loss of innocence/sanity. Honor that arc.

## Tone calibration

CoC is famously bleak. Discuss at session zero:
- **Pulp** (heroic, action-forward; investigators usually win): use heightened skills, more luck, fewer lethal scenes.
- **Classic** (tense, lethal, sanity-driven): standard rules.
- **Pure horror** (most investigators die or go insane): lethal lethality, mounting dread, no easy outs.

For most duet, **Classic** with occasional **Pulp** moments works well.

## Common Keeper mistakes to avoid

- **Front-loading lore.** The Mythos is meant to be discovered slowly. Don't dump it in the first scene.
- **Combat first.** CoC isn't D&D. Lead with investigation and unease.
- **Sanity penny-pinching.** Don't roll sanity for everything. Reserve for genuinely horrific moments.
- **Killer-Keeper lethality.** Telegraph deadly threats. Investigators should fear the unknown, not the GM.
- **Ignoring the system's safety nets.** Idea Rolls, Luck, Pushed Rolls are there for a reason. Use them.

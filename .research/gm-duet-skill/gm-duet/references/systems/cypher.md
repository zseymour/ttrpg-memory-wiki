# Cypher System / Numenera — System-Specific Guide

Covers Cypher System, Numenera, The Strange, Predation, Unmasked, Invisible Sun, and the wider Cypher family from Monte Cook Games.

## Core mechanic

**The GM never rolls.** The player does all rolling.

For any task with uncertainty:
1. GM sets **difficulty** 1–10.
2. Target number = difficulty × 3 (D5 = TN 15 on d20).
3. Player rolls d20; if ≥ TN, success.

The signature move: **the player lowers difficulty before rolling.** Through Skills, Assets, Effort, Edge, and helpful circumstances, the player reduces the difficulty step by step. By the time they roll, the task may be much easier.

## Difficulty steps and what they mean

| Diff | TN | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | — | Auto-success; don't roll |
| 1 | 3 | Routine for most |
| 2 | 6 | Simple |
| 3 | 9 | Standard |
| 4 | 12 | Demanding |
| 5 | 15 | Difficult |
| 6 | 18 | Challenging |
| 7 | 21 | Intimidating; requires effort/skills/assets to attempt |
| 8 | 24 | Formidable |
| 9 | 27 | Heroic |
| 10 | 30 | Impossible without significant help |

A PC can attempt difficulty 7+ only by lowering it via skills, effort, or assets first. Raw d20 ≥ 21 is not achievable.

## Lowering difficulty

The PC reduces difficulty before rolling through:

1. **Trained** in relevant skill: −1 step. **Specialized:** −2 steps.
2. **Asset** (gear, ally, environmental advantage): −1 step. Up to 2 assets per task.
3. **Effort:** spend points from the relevant Pool (Might / Speed / Intellect). 1 point of Effort = −1 step. PC's Edge reduces the Pool cost.
4. **GM-granted advantage** (clever fiction): −1 step.

A heroic PC with Specialized skill (−2), 1 asset (−1), and 2 levels of Effort (−2) reduces a difficulty 7 task to a difficulty 2 — TN 6, easy roll.

## GM Intrusions (the GM's only mechanical lever)

Since the GM doesn't roll, the GM injects complication via **Intrusions.**

How it works:
1. GM proposes a complication ("Your gun jams." "The bridge collapses." "The guard turns at the worst moment.").
2. Player can **accept** (gain 2 XP, give 1 to another player — solo: just keep both, or use the second to fuel a future intrusion refusal).
3. Player can **refuse** (spend 1 XP).

GM Intrusions should:
- Be appropriate to fiction (not "you trip for no reason").
- Be meaningful (matter to the scene).
- Hit at dramatic moments.
- Average ~1 per session per PC; more in tense scenes.

**Player Intrusions:** Player can spend 1 XP to introduce a beneficial complication ("The guard I'm fighting is a former friend who recognizes me").

## Cyphers (one-shot magic items / tech)

The signature element. Each PC carries 2–3 cyphers (capacity varies by class). Cyphers are:
- Single-use.
- Powerful (often dramatic — teleport, grant resistance, deal massive damage).
- Themed to setting (technological for Numenera, magical for Cypher fantasy, dimensional for The Strange).

### Cypher discipline:
- **Hand them out generously.** 1–2 per session is typical.
- **Encourage use.** Cyphers in pocket = cyphers wasted; the system wants them spent.
- **Random or chosen?** Numenera uses random cypher tables; later Cypher games allow choice. Either works.

### Subtle vs. anoetic:
- **Anoetic** cyphers: look like devices; clearly mysterious tech.
- **Occultic** cyphers: subtle, mysterious, often look mundane.
- **Subtle:** narrative effect rather than mechanical (a cypher of "luck" or "memory").

## Pools and Effort

PC has three Pools: Might (physical), Speed (agility), Intellect (mental).
- Pools are stat values AND HP.
- Damage drains pools (Might first usually).
- Spending Effort drains the relevant pool.

**The PC manages their pools strategically.** Early-encounter Effort drains pools needed for late-encounter survival. This creates resource tension throughout the day.

## Combat structure

Initiative: a Speed task vs. enemy level × 3.

A turn = action + 1 free move (or movement + restricted action). Actions:
- Attack.
- Defend (negate an incoming attack with a roll).
- Activate a power.
- Use a cypher.
- Move (additional, beyond free move).
- Help an ally (grants asset).

Defense rolls: Speed (dodge), Might (resist), or Intellect (mental defense), each vs. enemy level × 3.

## Recovery rolls

When the PC takes damage and is depleted:
- **One-action recovery:** +1d6+tier to one pool.
- **Ten-minute recovery:** restore more.
- **One-hour recovery.**
- **Ten-hour recovery (sleep):** full recovery.

Each is used once per day, in order. So a long session may see full recovery only with rest.

## XP economy

XP serves multiple purposes:
- **Refuse GM Intrusion** (1 XP).
- **Re-roll** (1 XP).
- **Long-term benefit** (training a skill, etc.).
- **Tier advancement** (4 XP, plus completed tier objectives).

Encourage players to spend XP. Hoarded XP doesn't help anyone.

## Tier and advancement

PCs are tiers 1–6. Advancement requires:
- 4 XP per advancement step.
- 4 advancement steps per tier.
- Specific accomplishments per tier (varies by game).

Tiers progress slowly compared to D&D levels. Plan for 4-6 sessions per tier.

## GM techniques specific to Cypher

### "GM doesn't roll" rhythm
Combat in Cypher feels different because the GM never rolls. NPC effects fire on PC failed defenses. NPC actions are GM Intrusion fuel.

The pacing is: PC rolls; PC narrates result; GM narrates consequence. The GM's job is to make the world dynamic without dice.

### Difficulty as the universal language
Every challenge is a difficulty number. Persuading a guard, climbing a wall, hacking a system — all are difficulty 1–10. Calibrate quickly: routine = 2; standard = 3; demanding = 4; expert = 5; difficult = 6; etc.

### The Effort budget
Track how PC pools are draining over the day. If pools are flush, raise difficulty. If pools are drained, lower difficulty (the PC is already strained).

### Cyphers as story drivers
A new cypher every session is a new tool. Sometimes seed cyphers thematically — the PC entering a haunted mansion finds a cypher of "see the dead." Sometimes random — the surprise is fun.

## Numenera-specific

Numenera is far-future post-apocalyptic where ancient tech reads as magic.

**Tone:** wonder + danger. Most discoveries are alien and incomprehensible. Don't over-explain.

**Numenera artifacts:** like cyphers but reusable, with a depletion roll after use. Track them between sessions.

**Discovery:** the heart of the game. Explore ruins; find things you don't understand; find ways to use them.

**The Ninth World setting:** earth one billion years in the future. Eight prior civilizations rose and fell, leaving incomprehensible relics. The PCs live in a medieval-ish present built on those ruins.

## Cypher for duet

### Strengths for duet
- Single PC works fine; no party-balance issues.
- Difficulty is GM-set, so trivially scalable.
- Cypher as one-shot resources gives the solo PC narrative leverage.
- GM Intrusions structure complications without fudging.

### Adjustments for duet
- Lower difficulty defaults (a solo PC can't tank tasks the same way a party can; default to Standard or Demanding rather than Difficult).
- Hand out cyphers more generously (the solo PC is the only one using them).
- Use GM Intrusions as your main pressure tool (rather than relying on enemy numbers).
- The PC Tier should advance somewhat faster in duet to compensate for lower party experience accumulation.

### Combat adjustments
- Lower enemy level by 1–2 from suggested.
- Use fewer enemies (1–2 vs. the duet PC).
- Make use of "minion" creatures (level 2 mooks the PC drops easily) to give the cinematic feel.

## Cypher for crunchy/narrative spectrum

Cypher is unusually flexible. It's mechanically straightforward (one resolution: roll d20 vs. TN) but narratively rich (cyphers, intrusions, descriptors and foci).

For crunchy preference: use Effort heavily, track pools tightly, run combat with careful action economy.

For narrative preference: hand out cyphers freely, use GM Intrusions as story beats, lean on the descriptor/type/focus PC framework as character.

## Common Cypher mistakes

- **Hoarding cyphers.** Encourage use. Cyphers are spent, not collected.
- **Underusing GM Intrusions.** They're your only pressure lever. Use 1–2 per session.
- **Forgetting Effort.** PCs should be spending Pool points routinely; if they're not, the system isn't engaging.
- **Static difficulty.** Adjust to fiction; what's hard depends on context.
- **Treating XP as gated.** Hand it out for accepting Intrusions, for cool play, for milestones.

## What to track between sessions

- Pool values (likely full after rest, but track if session ended mid-day).
- Cypher inventory (what's left; what depleted; what new cyphers found).
- Artifact depletion rolls (Numenera).
- XP earned.
- Tier progress.
- Discovered locations / artifacts / lore (huge in Numenera).

# Fate Core / Fate Accelerated — System-Specific Guide

Fate is a narrative system where **aspects** (descriptive phrases) drive both fiction and mechanics. Everything is a character; everything can have aspects.

## Resolution

**Core mechanic:** roll **4 Fate dice (4dF)** + skill rating vs. opposition.

Fate dice are six-sided with two `+`, two `-`, and two blanks. Total ranges -4 to +4, typically -1 to +2.

**Outcome:**
- **Fail** (lose by 3+): you don't accomplish your goal; pick: succeed at major cost, OR fail with minor consequence.
- **Tie** (within 1): succeed at minor cost, OR generate a boost.
- **Success** (beat by 1-2): accomplish your goal.
- **Success with Style** (beat by 3+): accomplish your goal AND extra benefit.

**Difficulty (passive opposition) ladder:**
- **0 (Mediocre):** trivial.
- **+1 (Average):** routine.
- **+2 (Fair):** standard.
- **+3 (Good):** challenging.
- **+4 (Great):** difficult.
- **+5 (Superb):** very hard.
- **+6 (Fantastic):** approaching limits.
- **+7 (Epic):** legendary.
- **+8 (Legendary).**

Default to +2 or +3 unless reason to deviate.

## Aspects (the heart of Fate)

An aspect is a short evocative phrase that's **always true** about a character, scene, or game.

### Types:
- **Character aspects:** PC's defining traits (5 by default — High Concept, Trouble, plus 3 others).
- **Situation aspects:** scene-bound (e.g., "On Fire," "Unstable Ground").
- **Game aspects:** campaign-bound (e.g., "The Empire Crumbles," "Magic is Forbidden").
- **Boost:** one-shot, free invoke, fades after use.
- **Consequence:** a wound or condition (Mild, Moderate, Severe).

### Aspects do work three ways:

1. **Established truth.** "Stuck in the Mud" means the PC is stuck — fictionally. Independent of dice.
2. **Invocations** (spend Fate Point to invoke for +2 or reroll). Player taps an aspect to gain a benefit.
3. **Compels** (GM offers, PC accepts FP + complication, OR refuses by spending FP). Drives drama.

### The Bronze Rule (Fractal)

**Anything in the game can be a character.** A city has aspects, skills, stunts. A storm has aspects. A sword has aspects. Apply the same mechanics to anything.

Useful in duet: scenes themselves can have aspect tracks. The "Burning Library" has stress; PC reduces it through actions.

## Compels

The most important GM tool in Fate.

**Compel:** GM offers a complication that fits an aspect. PC accepts → gains 1 Fate Point + complication occurs.

Two types:
- **Event compel:** something happens to the PC because of their aspect. ("Because you're a *Wanted Fugitive*, the bounty hunters across the bar have noticed you. Take a Fate Point.")
- **Decision compel:** the PC's aspect drives them to make a specific choice. ("Because you're *Loyal to a Fault*, you can't leave your friend behind, even though you should. Take a Fate Point.")

**PC can refuse** by spending 1 Fate Point — but should rarely refuse. Compels are the FP economy's lifeblood.

**Compel often.** Several per session. Compels:
- Reward player for engaging with their aspects.
- Drive drama.
- Refresh the FP economy.
- Make aspects matter.

## Fate Points (FP)

The currency of Fate. Players spend to:
- **Invoke an aspect** for +2 or reroll.
- **Declare a story detail** (with GM agreement). "There's a vent here — I'm a security expert; I'd know."
- **Power stunts** (some stunts cost FP).
- **Refuse a compel.**

Players gain FP by:
- **Accepting a compel.**
- **Defeated by their own aspect** (unwillingly act on it).
- **Refresh** at start of session (default 3 FP refresh).

GM has FP too — typically 1 per PC, refreshed per scene. Spend on NPC invokes, NPC declarations.

**Aim for FP economy churn.** If players are hoarding, compel more. If they're broke, give more opportunities to gain.

## Skills

Default skill list (Fate Core): Athletics, Burglary, Contacts, Crafts, Deceive, Drive, Empathy, Fight, Investigate, Lore, Notice, Physique, Provoke, Rapport, Resources, Shoot, Stealth, Will. (Customizable per setting.)

**Pyramid distribution at character creation:** 1 at Great (+4), 2 at Good (+3), 3 at Fair (+2), 4 at Average (+1), rest at Mediocre (+0).

**Skill use modes:**
- **Overcome:** beat passive obstacle.
- **Create an Advantage:** create a temporary aspect (Boost, or scene aspect with free invokes).
- **Attack:** deal stress.
- **Defend:** opposed roll vs. attack.

## Conflicts (combat or social)

**Stress and Consequences** instead of HP.

**Stress boxes:** typically 3 (Physical) and 3 (Mental) by default. Tick a box to absorb damage equal to the box's value (1-3 stress).

**Consequences:** when stress is insufficient, take a Consequence (an aspect that hurts you):
- **Mild** (-2 stress equivalent): minor; lasts 1 scene.
- **Moderate** (-4 stress equivalent): persistent; lasts the session.
- **Severe** (-6 stress equivalent): major; takes a scenario+ to heal.

**Taken Out:** conceded all stress and consequences = defeated. Loser narrates the loss (within reason). Winner sets the scene's outcome.

**Concession:** at any point, voluntarily concede the conflict. Receive Fate Points based on consequences taken. The character isn't taken out — they exit on their terms (mostly). This is huge for keeping characters alive while still losing.

## Contests and Challenges

Beyond conflicts, Fate has structured non-combat resolutions:

- **Contest:** opposed; whoever scores 3 victories first wins. Used for chases, races, debates.
- **Challenge:** series of overcome rolls representing a complex task. Each addresses one facet.

Both are great for non-combat tension.

## Fate Accelerated (lighter variant)

Replaces 18 skills with 6 **Approaches**: Careful, Clever, Flashy, Forceful, Quick, Sneaky. Player picks the approach they're using; same resolution mechanic.

Faster, lighter, narrative-forward. Recommended for duet — less skill-list bookkeeping.

## Aspects in duet

In duet, aspects do extra work:
- **PC aspects** are the engine of compels — drives the protagonist's arc.
- **Scene aspects** that you create through Create-an-Advantage make the world feel rich.
- **NPC aspects** drive their behavior — "Loyal to the Duke," "Hates the Cult."
- **Story aspects** anchor the campaign theme.

Compel often. Aspects unused are wasted.

## Running Fate in duet

Fate scales naturally to one PC. Adjustments:

- **One PC = full FP economy on them.** They get more compels, more invokes.
- **Reduce nameless NPCs to "mooks"** (one stress box, no consequences) for efficiency.
- **Important NPCs get full stress tracks and aspects** (and may have FP of their own).
- **Story aspects** anchor the duet's themes — make 2-3 explicit.
- **The Bronze Rule scales beautifully:** treat the city / faction / storm as a character with stress.

## What Fate does that other systems don't

- **Aspects always-on.** They're permission and constraint, not just bonuses.
- **Compels formalize "your character would do X."** Making BIT engagement mechanical.
- **The fractal.** Anything is a character.
- **Concession** — the elegant alternative to dying.
- **Player narrative authority.** Declare story details with FP.

## Common Fate mistakes

- **Forgetting to compel.** The system starves without compels.
- **Treating aspects as numerical bonuses.** They're permissions and truths first.
- **Calling for too many rolls.** Aspects can resolve fictionally without dice.
- **Not creating advantages.** Aspects-on-the-scene are huge; encourage Create an Advantage.
- **Ignoring the fractal.** Treating only PCs as characters wastes the system's flexibility.
- **Forgetting concession.** PCs don't have to die; they can concede.

## Fate setup checklist

- 5 PC aspects: High Concept, Trouble, plus 3 others.
- Skill pyramid (or Approaches for FAE).
- 3 stunts (specific exceptional abilities).
- Stress + Consequence tracks.
- 3 FP refresh per session.
- Game-level aspects (1-3, defined at session zero).

## Common social skills in Fate

Fate handles social conflict mechanically. Don't shortcut to "you persuaded them."

- **Rapport:** sincere persuasion, trust-building.
- **Deceive:** lying, manipulation.
- **Provoke:** intimidation, antagonism.
- **Empathy:** reading people, defending against social attacks.

Social attacks deal Mental stress. NPCs can be Taken Out socially — concede to your argument, become an ally, leave the scene.

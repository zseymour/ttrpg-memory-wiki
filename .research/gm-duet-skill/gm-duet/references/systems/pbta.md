# Powered by the Apocalypse (PbtA) — System-Specific Guide

Covers Apocalypse World, Dungeon World, Masks, Monster of the Week, The Veil, Urban Shadows, and the broader PbtA family. Specific games vary, but the core architecture is consistent.

## Resolution

**Core mechanic:** roll 2d6 + relevant stat (typically -1 to +3).

- **10+:** full success.
- **7-9:** partial / mixed success — succeed at a cost, succeed but with complications, choose-from-a-list.
- **6-:** miss. **GM makes a hard move.**
- **12+ (in some games):** critical / extra benefit.

The GM does not roll. **Only the player rolls, and the player only rolls when they trigger a move.**

## Moves are the engine

Players have a list of **moves** — specific labeled actions with mechanical effects. The fiction triggers the move. Movement → roll → effect.

Examples (Dungeon World):
- **Hack and Slash:** when you attack in melee, roll +Str.
- **Volley:** when you take aim and shoot at someone at range, roll +Dex.
- **Defy Danger:** when you act despite an imminent threat, roll vs. relevant stat.
- **Spout Lore:** when you consult your accumulated knowledge, roll +Int.
- **Discern Realities:** when you closely study a situation or person, roll +Wis.
- **Parley:** when you have leverage on a GM character and try to get them to do something, roll +Cha.

**Critical:** the fiction must justify the move. If the player says "I attack," but they're 50 ft away and unarmed, Hack and Slash doesn't trigger. They must establish the fictional position first.

## Beginning and ending with the fiction

PbtA's mantra. Every move is wrapped in description.

**Bad:** "Roll Hack and Slash. ... 8. Pick: deal damage, take damage, or expose yourself to danger."

**Good:** "You charge the orc, axe high. *Roll Hack and Slash.* ... 8. The orc twists; your blade slices but he's already swinging back — you can finish him but eat the counter, or back off and re-set. What do you do?"

The mechanics are punctuation in a sentence the fiction speaks.

## The GM's moves

The GM has their own list of moves. **Use them when:**
- The player rolls a 6– (miss).
- The player gives a "golden opportunity" (walks into something, ignores a soft setup).
- Everyone looks to the GM to find out what happens.

### The basic GM move list (Dungeon World version, generalizable):

1. Use a monster, danger, or location move.
2. Reveal an unwelcome truth.
3. Show signs of an approaching threat.
4. Deal damage.
5. Use up their resources.
6. Turn their move back on them.
7. Separate them.
8. Give an opportunity that fits a class' ability.
9. Show a downside to their class, race, or equipment.
10. Offer an opportunity, with or without cost.
11. Put someone in a spot.
12. Tell them the requirements or consequences and ask.

### Soft vs. hard moves

- **Soft move:** no immediate irrevocable consequence. Sets up; gives the player a chance to react. Use on 7-9 partials, look-to-you moments.
- **Hard move:** immediate, irrevocable consequence. Use on 6– misses, golden opportunities, or when a soft move was ignored.

A soft move ignored becomes a golden opportunity for a hard move. **Default to the softest move that still moves the fiction.**

## The cardinal rule

**Never speak the name of your move.** Don't say "I'm using a soft move to show signs of an approaching threat." Just say "Footsteps in the corridor. Slow. Then stopped."

The player should experience moves as fiction, not mechanics.

## GM principles

The standard PbtA GM agenda + principles (varies by game; this is the Dungeon World list, broadly applicable):

### Agenda:
1. Portray a fantastic world.
2. Fill the characters' lives with adventure.
3. Play to find out what happens.

### Principles:
1. Draw maps, leave blanks.
2. Address the characters, not the players.
3. Embrace the fantastic.
4. Make a move that follows.
5. Never speak the name of your move.
6. Give every monster life.
7. Name every person.
8. Ask questions and use the answers.
9. Be a fan of the characters.
10. Think dangerous.
11. Begin and end with the fiction.
12. Think offscreen too.

These aren't optional flavor. They're the operational ethos.

## "Be a fan of the characters"

You are not adversarial to the PCs. You are a fan rooting for them, who happens to also be running the world they live in. The world is dangerous; the world is honest; but you want the PC to succeed in their goals.

This stance is critical in duet, where it scales naturally.

## Ask questions and use the answers

Build the world from player input. Default move when introducing something:

- "What does this place remind you of?"
- "Who's the most dangerous person here?"
- "When you hear that, what comes to mind?"
- "You're a wizard? Who taught you?"

Use the answers as canon. The player's contributions become the world.

## "What do you do?"

The default question. End every soft move with it. End every description with it. The player's response drives the fiction.

## Fronts (the long-form prep system)

A **front** is a coherent set of related threats, motivated by a fundamental scarcity, pointing toward a **dark future** if unchecked.

Each front:
- **Name** — evocative.
- **Driving force** — the underlying motivation (greed, hunger, vengeance).
- **Threats** (2–4 specific dangers).
- **Dark future** — what happens if PCs don't intervene (3 / 6 / 12 months out).
- **Stakes questions** — what you genuinely want to find out via play.
- **Custom moves** — specific to this front's threats (optional).

Run 1–2 adventure fronts (immediate, current arc) + 1–2 campaign fronts (long-term, slow-burn).

See `references/campaign-structure.md` for full front mechanics.

## Dungeon World specifics

If running Dungeon World specifically:

- **Last Breath:** when you're at 0 HP, you make a deal with Death. 10+ = cheat death; 7-9 = bargain (Death names a price); 6- = die.
- **Bonds:** characters have Bonds with each other. Resolved bonds grant XP. (In duet: PC has bonds with NPCs and family; resolve through play.)
- **End of Session XP:** ask three questions. "Did we learn something new and important about the world?" "Did we overcome a notable monster or enemy?" "Did we loot a memorable treasure?" Mark XP per yes.

## Apocalypse World specifics

- **MC moves are sharper / harder.** The world is brutal.
- **Hx (history) tracks** between PCs.
- **Threats are formalized:** Warlords, Grotesques, Brutes, Afflictions, Landscapes, Maelstroms — each with their own moves.
- **The Maelstrom** is a psychic environment. Open Brain rolls reveal information.

## Masks specifics

- **Conditions** instead of HP: Afraid, Angry, Guilty, Hopeless, Insecure. Each gives -2 to certain rolls.
- **Influence** between characters and adults. Characters can shift Labels (identity stats) when given Influence by others.
- **The teen-superhero focus:** drama is identity, not lethality.

## Monster of the Week specifics

- **The Investigation phase** uses specific moves (Investigate a Mystery, Read a Bad Situation).
- **Monsters have a weakness.** PC must discover it through investigation.
- **Mysteries have countdowns** (Day, Sundown, Sunset, Nightfall, Midnight, Dawn). Each step is a deadline.

## Running PbtA in duet

PbtA is exceptionally well-suited to duet because:
- Mechanics drive collaborative storytelling.
- The "what do you do?" loop is naturally tight.
- Asking questions is built into the system.
- One PC = full spotlight in the system that already gives full spotlight per move.

Adjustments:
- **No party Hx rolls.** PC tracks Hx with NPCs only.
- **More opportunities for soft moves to land.** No party to absorb consequences.
- **Stakes are personal.** The dark futures impact the PC directly.
- **End of session XP questions** can be tweaked for solo arc.

## Common PbtA mistakes

- **Calling for rolls without triggering moves.** If the action doesn't trigger a move, don't roll. Move forward fictionally.
- **Asking the player to "roll for X" without identifying the move.** The player declares; you confirm the move triggered.
- **Skipping the "ask questions" step.** Without player input, your world is one-sided.
- **Speaking the name of the move.** Never. The player should experience fiction.
- **Soft-pedaling 6- misses.** A miss means hard consequences. Land them.
- **Ignoring the principles.** They're the operating system, not a vibe.

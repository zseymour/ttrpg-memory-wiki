# Year Zero Engine — System-Specific Guide

Covers Forbidden Lands, Alien, The One Ring 2e (similar lineage), Mutant: Year Zero, Coriolis, Vaesen, Tales from the Loop, Twilight 2000, Blade Runner. Free League's signature system.

## Core mechanic

**d6 dice pool.** Roll dice equal to **Attribute + Skill + Gear**. Any 6 = a success.

- **0 successes:** failure.
- **1 success:** standard success.
- **Multiple sixes:** bonus effects, depending on system (called "stunts" in Forbidden Lands).

## The Push

The signature mechanic. After a failed roll, the PC may **push** — re-roll all dice that didn't show 1 or 6, but pay a thematic cost.

Cost varies by game:
- **Forbidden Lands:** Damage to attribute (Strength, Agility, Wits, Empathy depending on roll).
- **Alien:** Stress increases (one die added to next roll, but each Stress die can trigger a Panic Roll on a 1).
- **Coriolis:** Darkness Points to the GM (use for complications).
- **Mutant: Year Zero:** mutation-driven complications.
- **Vaesen:** Conditions (Exhausted, Hungry, Cold, Scared).
- **The One Ring:** Weariness.

The Push is dramatic. **Encourage pushing** — the cost is what makes the game thematic.

## Critical successes / failures

- **Multiple 6s:** stunts. Extra effect; system-specific.
- **1s on attribute dice when pushing:** "trauma" effect — damage to the attribute.

## Setting difficulty

Year Zero doesn't use DCs. The pool size IS the difficulty calibration.

- **Routine:** small pool sufficient.
- **Hard:** average attribute alone, no skill.
- **Very Hard:** attribute - 1 die from circumstance.

Modifiers are typically **+1 to -3** dice based on situation.

## Combat

Combat varies by game but shares structure:

- **Initiative:** card-based or quick d10 (game-dependent).
- **Action + Move per round.**
- **Attack:** dice pool roll; each 6 = damage success.
- **Damage:** based on weapon. Reduce target's attribute (Strength typically).
- **Critical injuries:** when an attribute hits 0, roll on a critical injury table — persistent wounds, may kill, may scar.

**Critical injuries persist.** They're a major thematic element. The PC accumulates wounds over a campaign.

## Specific systems

### Forbidden Lands

**The OSR-flavored YZE.** Hex-crawl; old-school resource management; brutal.

#### Quarter Day journey procedure
- Day divided into 4 watches: Morning, Day, Evening, Night.
- Each watch, PCs assign roles:
  - **Leader:** chooses direction.
  - **Pathfinder:** Survival check; if fails, lost / wandering monster.
  - **Hunter:** can hunt for food.
  - **Cook:** prepares food (if available); morale boost on success.
  - **Lookout:** Scouting on rest; spots threats.
- Roll for encounters per terrain.
- Track food, water, exhaustion.

This is the system's overland exploration engine. Use it for any wilderness travel.

#### Pushing in Forbidden Lands
Damages attributes (Strength for physical; Wits for mental; etc.). Damage heals over time but stacks dangerously.

### Alien

**Sci-fi horror.** Stress-driven; Panic-driven.

#### Stress and Panic
Stress accumulates from horror, danger, exhaustion. Each stress = 1 extra die added to rolls (good, except for the 1s).

When a Stress die rolls 1, **Panic Roll:** 1d6 + current Stress level.
- Below 7: handle it; minor reaction.
- 7+: escalating reactions — Tremble → Drop item → Freeze → Berserk → Catatonic.

**Panic spreads.** A character at high Panic forces nearby characters to make Stress rolls.

#### The Cinematic mode
Pre-built scenarios with three acts. Each act has Stress consequences. Mortality is high — characters often die in cinematic play.

#### The Campaign mode
Persistent characters; longer arcs; survivable but brutal.

### The One Ring 2e

**Tolkien-flavored.** Hope + Shadow as moral compass.

#### Hope and Shadow
Hope is the PC's positive resource — spend for bonus dice, healing, resisting Shadow.
Shadow accumulates from exposure to evil (Shadow Points). Too much Shadow = the PC succumbs (becomes corrupted, retires, or worse).

#### Adventuring Phase / Fellowship Phase
Sessions alternate:
- **Adventuring Phase:** quest activity. Standard play.
- **Fellowship Phase:** between adventures. PC heals, builds relationships, undertakes long-term projects (heirlooms, knowledge, alliances).

This rhythm structures campaigns. Use it.

#### Journeys
Formal procedure for travel. Roles: Guide, Look-out, Hunter, Scout. Each makes their roll; weather and terrain affect difficulty. Roll for journey events; resolve.

### Mutant: Year Zero

**Post-apocalyptic.** PCs are mutants in a ruined world.

- Mutations: special abilities; trigger Mutation Points; failure can corrupt.
- The Ark: home base; survival vs. resource scarcity.
- Sectors: explore the ruined world hex by hex.

### Coriolis: The Third Horizon

**Arabesque space opera.** PCs are Crew of a Ship in a mystical sci-fi setting.

- Darkness Points: GM currency (gain when PCs Push); spend to add complications, NPC bonuses, environmental hazards.
- Praying to Icons: spend Reputation for divine intervention (limited per session).

### Vaesen

**Nordic gothic horror.** PCs are members of a Society investigating supernatural beings.

- Investigation-focused.
- Conditions: Exhausted, Hungry, Cold, Sleepy, Hungry, Thirsty, Scared. Each gives -1 die.
- Vaesen (the monsters): each has a specific weakness; ritual-banishment more common than fighting.

### Tales from the Loop / Things from the Flood

**80s nostalgia + sci-fi.** PCs are kids investigating mysteries.

- No combat-killing; PCs cannot die.
- Pride, Anchors, Drives anchor characters.
- Strong character-driven; lighter mechanics.

Excellent for duet — character-focused, low-lethal, high-mystery.

### Twilight 2000

**Modern military survival.** Hex-crawl with vehicles, supplies, NATO collapsing.

- Realistic combat; lethal.
- Encumbrance, fuel, supplies.
- Hex-crawl with travel events.

## Running YZE in duet

YZE scales well to duet. Key considerations:

- **One PC's Push costs hit harder** because there's no party to compensate. Use stress / damage costs as character development moments.
- **The journey procedures** (Forbidden Lands quarter days, One Ring journeys) work for solo travel — PC takes all the roles or selects one per watch.
- **Critical injuries persist** and shape the campaign — lean into them as character-shaping events.
- **Specific systems are duet-friendly:**
  - **Vaesen** for solo gothic investigators.
  - **The One Ring** for hobbit-scale solo adventures.
  - **Tales from the Loop** for solo kid-mystery.
  - **Alien** in cinematic mode for one-shot horror.

## Common YZE mistakes

- **Discouraging Push.** The system *wants* pushed rolls. They're dramatic.
- **Forgetting attribute damage.** Push consequences must land mechanically.
- **Skipping the journey procedure.** It's the system's exploration engine.
- **Treating critical injuries as flavor.** They have mechanical effects; remember them.
- **Ignoring system-specific subsystems.** Each YZE game has its signature mechanic (Stress in Alien, Hope in One Ring, etc.). Use them.

## What YZE does well

- **Resource attrition** as drama. Each push costs; each cost shapes character.
- **Procedural exploration** (journey systems).
- **Setting-specific mechanics** that reinforce theme.
- **Lethal but recoverable.** PCs take wounds and persist; death is meaningful when it happens.

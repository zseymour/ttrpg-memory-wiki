# The Craft of Game Mastering: A Comprehensive Source Document for an AI GM Skill

This document synthesizes the most-cited GMing techniques across the tabletop community, organized for direct use as source material for a system-agnostic AI GM skill optimized for 1-on-1 (duet) play. Every section emphasizes concrete, deployable procedures over abstract philosophy.

---

## 1. THE MASTER PRINCIPLES (cross-tradition consensus)

These ~15 mantras recur across every major school — OSR, modern 5e, PbtA, FitD, Fate, Burning Wheel, Forge — and form the spine of any AI GM skill.

1. **"Play to find out what happens."** (Apocalypse World; ubiquitous in indie design.) The GM does not pre-script outcomes. Outcomes emerge from PC choices + dice + the GM's honest application of fiction-following moves.
2. **"Be a fan of the player's character."** (Apocalypse World; Cypher; modern 5e advice.) The GM is not the player's adversary. The world is dangerous; the GM is rooting for the protagonist.
3. **"Prep situations, not plots."** (Justin Alexander, "Don't Prep Plots," 2008.) Stat the antagonists, locations, factions, timelines-if-PCs-do-nothing. Never script "and then they will…"
4. **"Address the character, not the player."** (Dungeon World GM principle.) Speak in second person to the PC: "Dunwick, what do you do?" not "Tony, what is your guy doing?"
5. **"Begin and end with the fiction."** (Dungeon World.) Mechanics are the bridge; never enter or exit dice-resolution naked. Frame every mechanic as a fictional event.
6. **"Never speak the name of your move."** (Dungeon World.) Never say "I'm using the 'put someone in a spot' move." Describe what happens in the world.
7. **"Ask questions and use the answers."** (Apocalypse World.) Build the world from player input. "What does your village look like? Who taught you the old songs?"
8. **"Draw maps, leave blanks."** (Dungeon World.) Specify enough to be real; leave gaps to be filled in collaboratively.
9. **"Rulings, not rules."** (Gygax 1979 DMG; OSR.) When rules don't cover a case, decide quickly with common sense, log the ruling, move on.
10. **"Roll dice when the outcome is uncertain AND the consequences of failure are interesting."** (5e DMG paraphrase; near-universal.) Otherwise narrate.
11. **"Don't roll if you can't accept the result."** (Sly Flourish; modern consensus.) Don't fudge; instead, don't call for rolls whose outcomes you wouldn't honor.
12. **"Telegraph everything."** (Angry GM; OSR; Matt Colville.) Players need information to make meaningful choices. Foreshadow danger 2–3 times before triggering it.
13. **"Failure must move the fiction forward."** (PbtA, Fate, Blades, Burning Wheel.) Never "you fail; nothing happens." Failure produces complication, cost, or escalation.
14. **"Think offscreen too."** (Dungeon World; Blades faction clocks.) The world acts when the PCs aren't watching. Factions advance their own goals between sessions.
15. **"You cannot have a meaningful campaign if strict time records are not kept."** (Gary Gygax, AD&D DMG p. 37.) Track the calendar; downtime, faction clocks, deadlines all depend on it.

## 2. THE GM'S OPERATING LOOP

Every system layers the same core loop. An AI GM should run this constantly:

1. **Describe the situation.** Frame what the PC perceives — sensory, terse, actionable.
2. **Receive the player's declaration.** What does the PC try? What's the **intent** (goal) and **approach** (method)?
3. **Adjudicate.** Can it succeed? Can it fail? Is there a real cost? If all three: call for a roll. Else: narrate result directly.
4. **Resolve fictionally.** Translate any mechanical result back into fiction. Show consequences.
5. **Make a move when the players look to you.** Apply soft moves on partial success or look-to-you moments; hard moves on misses, golden opportunities, or ignored soft moves.
6. **End with "What do you do?"**

This loop is from Apocalypse World/Dungeon World structurally and mirrors Angry GM's "Declare → Determine → Describe" cycle and Bastionland's "Information → Choice → Impact" doctrine.

## 3. THE LAZY DM 8-STEP PREP (Sly Flourish / Mike Shea)

The dominant modern prep framework. *Return of the Lazy Dungeon Master* (2018).

1. **Review the characters** — names, goals, backstories, what each player enjoys.
2. **Create a strong start** — the opening scene that drops the PC into immediate action and a choice within minutes. After the recap, immediately put something in motion: an alarm, an NPC's demand, a discovery. No "you sit at the inn…"
3. **Outline potential scenes** — ~one per 45 min of play, in bullets. Hold loosely.
4. **Define secrets and clues** — write **10 short, location-agnostic facts** the PCs *might* discover. Each 1–2 sentences. Drop them into whatever scene fits — overheard, found, divined, told. Carry unused secrets forward. **This is the heart of lazy prep.**
5. **Develop fantastic locations** — 3–5 evocative places. Each: an evocative title (e.g., "The Sunspire") + **three fantastic aspects** (e.g., "moat of molten rock," "glyphs in dead Elvish," "lightning humming in the air").
6. **Outline important NPCs** — name + one-line motivation/role + a hook.
7. **Choose relevant monsters** — list with stat block references; pre-familiarize.
8. **Select magic-item rewards** — pre-pick a few items themed to characters; deploy when situational.

**Minimum viable prep (3-step):** Strong Start + 10 Secrets/Clues + 3 Fantastic Locations.

**The key insight (Shea):** "The value isn't the notes but the step-by-step procedure that gets your head around the game before you run it." Prep is a thinking aid that produces table-side improvisation; not a script.

## 4. ADJUDICATION — WHEN TO CALL FOR A ROLL

The flagship procedure for any GM. Synthesized from the Angry GM, Sly Flourish, the 5e DMG, and OSR consensus.

### 4.1 Intent + Approach (Angry GM canonical procedure)

1. Player declares **Intent** (what they want to accomplish) and **Approach** (how). If they only state intent ("I want past the guard"), ask "How?" before resolving.
2. GM checks three conditions — **all must be true** to call for a roll:
   - Can it succeed?
   - Can it fail?
   - Is there a meaningful cost or consequence to failure?
3. If yes-all-three → roll.
4. If success is automatic → just say yes.
5. If failure is impossible or no consequence → just say yes.
6. If success is impossible → just say no, with a fictional reason.
7. **Intent determines the outcome; approach determines the consequences.** Picking the lock vs. smashing it: same outcome (door opens), different consequences (quiet vs. loud).
8. Describe in fiction.

### 4.2 DC ladders (5e default; adapt by system)

| Difficulty | 5e DC | PbtA | Blades |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auto-easy | (no roll) | (no roll) | (no roll) |
| Easy | 10 | 7–9 partial | Risky w/ Great effect |
| Medium | 15 | 10+ full | Risky w/ Standard effect |
| Hard | 20 | (10+ vs. high stat) | Desperate w/ Standard |
| Very Hard | 25 | (rare) | Desperate w/ Limited |
| Nigh-impossible | 30 | (rare) | (rare) |

**Sly Flourish simplification:** Use only 10, 15, or 20 in 5e. Set DC before the roll, never after.

### 4.3 Fail forward — the 5-option toolkit

When a roll fails AND stalling kills the scene, choose one or more:

1. **Succeed at a cost** (HP, exhaustion, resource burn).
2. **Game complication** (gear breaks, ammo out, lockpick snaps).
3. **Story complication** (alarm raised, NPC suspicious, faction angered).
4. **Raise the stakes** (a clock advances; time has passed; the situation worsens).
5. **Charge for success** (you make it but lose your rope and pitons).

**Rule:** Never let a single roll stop the plot. Either the door opens (eventually) or failure means it opens AND something else.

### 4.4 Cleverness rewarded

If the player's described approach is genuinely clever (exploits a real fictional feature), **just succeed**. If clever-but-uncertain, grant **advantage** (5e) or +1d (Blades) or boost (Fate) instead of auto-success. Don't punish creativity.

### 4.5 The fudging debate — what an AI GM should do

**Modern consensus: don't fudge dice.** Public rolls are the default discipline. The principle: "If you don't want to abide by the result, don't roll."

- **Anti-fudge** (Sly Flourish, Angry GM, OSR, indie/narrative community, Justin Alexander, Adam Koebel): Fudging breaks the social contract. Wins feel hollow; losses feel arbitrary.
- **Pro-fudge limited** (Matt Colville, 5e DMG p. 235): Fudge when system math produces a flat outcome (e.g., one-shot crit kills the buildup villain).
- **The defensible middle** (Sly Flourish): Fudge *prep numbers* (HP, encounter strength) before they hit the table; never fudge *post-roll*.

**For an AI GM: roll openly; pre-tune difficulty; never override results post-roll. If a result would ruin the scene, don't have called for the roll in the first place.**

## 5. DESCRIPTION & NARRATION

### 5.1 Rule of Three Senses
Every location description hits **at least two non-visual senses** plus sight. Default formula: **sight + sound + smell**. Add temperature/touch/taste in extreme environments. Sight-only descriptions are the #1 beginner mistake.

### 5.2 Rule of Three Details / Three Fantastic Aspects (Sly Flourish, Alexandrian)
Each location: an evocative two-word title + exactly three concrete details. Example: "Bloody Torture Chamber / blood-rusted iron maiden / wax-stub candles still warm / chains hanging through ceiling holes." Three details > one paragraph; players probe what they care about, and details *imply* more world without spending words.

### 5.3 Angry GM scene-setting recipe
For any scene: **what's the goal, what's in the way, what tools are at hand, the exits.** Five short sentences max. "Scene setting is invitation to act, not invitation to listen to your prose."

### 5.4 Second-person framing always
"You see," "you hear," "you smell." Pulls the player into the body. Avoid third-person omniscient ("the room contains a chest") which sounds like reading a module.

### 5.5 Show don't tell
Don't say "the duke is angry." Say "the duke's face flushes; his glass cracks in his fist." Let players infer; drop one observable detail and let questions pull more out of you.

### 5.6 One striking image
Every scene leads with ONE memorable, strange image. Not "a tavern" — "a tavern where every drink is served in a hollowed skull."

### 5.7 Specific concrete nouns
"Rosewood lute" beats "musical instrument." "Copper-and-glass orrery" beats "thing on the desk." Test: if a noun could be replaced by a more specific noun, do it.

### 5.8 Begin and end with the fiction (Dungeon World)
Dice never enter or exit naked. Wrap mechanical events in story: "You reach for the orc's neck — *roll Hack and Slash* — your blade slices through; he gurgles into the brazier."

### 5.9 Brief vs. linger
- **Brief (one sentence):** transit rooms, NPCs the players won't engage.
- **Linger (3-detail rule + sensory):** the boss's lair, dramatic reveals, places that touch a PC's backstory, decision points.
- **Read the table:** if the player is leaning forward, keep going. If they're checking phones, cut.

### 5.10 Sensory description for horror
- Lean on **smell** (rot, copper, ozone) — most subconscious, rarely used elsewhere.
- Use **sound voids** ("the well goes silent. You can't hear your companion") more than loud noises.
- Describe **what is missing or wrong** rather than what's there ("there are no rats. There should be rats").
- Give the villain a **signature sound** the PC learns to fear.
- Linger on a *single* horrifying detail one extra beat — then cut.

## 6. NPCs

### 6.1 Two-trait NPC (the workhorse)
For every walk-on NPC: 2 appearance adjectives + 2 personality adjectives + 1 goal/want. Total: 4 words and a sentence. Example: "burly, scruffy / considerate, cautious / saving for grandmother's healer."

### 6.2 Three-trait deep NPC: Desire / Fear / Weakness
For recurring NPCs and villains: one **desire** (concrete or abstract), one **fear**, one **weakness**. Desire vs. fear creates dramatic tension; weakness creates exploitable hooks.

### 6.3 The five voice levers
Pick at least ONE per NPC; never duplicate the same lever for two NPCs:
1. **Pitch register** (high/mid/low). Easiest, no accent risk.
2. **Cadence** (clipped, drawling, whispered, paused).
3. **Vocabulary register** (archaic vs. slang; long words vs. monosyllables).
4. **Speech tic / signature phrase** ("Aye, just so." Refers to PC as "my friend." Speaks in third person.)
5. **Posture/physicality** (lean forward, sip lazily, gesture with hands).

**Ginny Di's rule:** Exaggerate ONE characteristic; build voice from a posture and a single tell. Don't attempt accents you can't sustain.

### 6.4 Want vs. Appearance gap (Dungeon World "give every monster life")
Always write what the NPC *wants* separately from how they *appear*. The gap is the hook. "Wants: keep her son out of the war. Appears: cheerful innkeeper offering free ale to soldiers."

### 6.5 Stephen King's name rule (via Shea)
Never start two NPC names with the same letter in the same arc. Maintain a name file of 100+ names; cross out as used.

### 6.6 NPC continuity tracker
- **Name file:** alphabetized, each NPC has location, status (alive/dead/missing), 2 traits, last interaction, voice tag.
- **Relationship map:** who-knows-whom, ally/neutral/rival/enemy.
- **Who-knows-what tracker:** NPC × secret matrix. Critical for mysteries.
- **NPC downtime log:** what each recurring NPC did between sessions.

### 6.7 The villain
- Has a *reason* for being who they are (history, wound, ideology).
- Believes they are doing the right thing — and is *actually right* about something.
- Has plans that advance offscreen via **faction clocks**.
- Is intelligent.

**Villain monologue rule:** Under 30 seconds. Reveals one new fact. Lands a personal jab tied to PC backstory. Ends on an action — never on the speech.

**Recurring villain — letting them escape (5 methods):**
1. Telepresence appearance (sending, scrying, illusion).
2. Pre-set escape route (teleport circle, trapdoor, summoned exit).
3. Decoy/projected image (the "kill" was illusion).
4. Minion buffer (3–4 lackeys; villain monologues and exits behind them).
5. Consume a resource (potion, charge, artifact). **Crucial:** make escape *cost* the villain something visible — bleeding, scarring, a broken artifact left behind. Even unwon fights must show villain *losing something*.

**Best practice:** Disclose at session zero that recurring villains may escape. Avoids player frustration.

### 6.8 Companion / Sidekick NPCs (critical for duet)
- Use the **5e Sidekick** (Tasha's): Expert, Spellcaster, Warrior. CR ½ or lower. Levels with party.
- **Padawan model:** PC apprentices to a stronger NPC who eventually equals them. Avoids "the NPC keeps saving me" feel.
- **Run them after the PC, never before.** Never let the sidekick solve problems; they react.
- Give a **personal goal that may diverge** from the PC's — built-in story friction.
- Never let the sidekick outshine the PC in their specialty (rogue PC → not-a-rogue sidekick).
- Test: did the sidekick do this, or did the PC?

## 7. ENCOUNTER DESIGN

### 7.1 Beyond "kill them all" — the objective catalog
Every encounter answers a **dramatic question** (Angry GM): a yes/no the encounter resolves. Encounter ends when the question is answered.

Objectives beyond elimination:
- Retrieve / smash-and-grab (time pressure).
- Defend / hold the line (N rounds).
- Escort (VIP cowers).
- Escape (surrounded; exit is the goal).
- Race / chase.
- Capture alive.
- Disable / stop the ritual (clock).
- Demoralize / rout (use morale rules).
- Manipulate terrain (collapse bridge, flood room).

**Per-encounter checklist:** What's the win state? Fail state? Timer/threshold? Is this telegraphed in the opening description?

### 7.2 Terrain and interactables
At least 2–3 interactable features per encounter: chandelier to drop, brazier to push, ledge to shove off, choke point, cover. Objects players can manipulate beat raw HP attrition.

### 7.3 Action-oriented monsters (Matt Colville, *Flee, Mortals!*)
For solo bosses against a party (or a duet PC with sidekick):
1. **Double or triple HP** of the base stat block (don't raise AC — players need hits to land).
2. Add **at least one movement action** that doesn't provoke (escape disengagement traps).
3. Add **2+ reactions** triggered by likely PC behavior.
4. Add **3 Villain Actions**, one per round, on initiative + 20:
   - Round 1: setup/positioning/summon.
   - Round 2: battlefield-shaping.
   - Round 3: ultimate/desperation.
5. Optional: legendary resistance (1–3/day) so a single spell doesn't shut down the boss.

### 7.4 Telegraph monster abilities (Colville)
Before a monster uses a special ability, describe a precursor: bones in ash before the dragon's breath; a rumble before the bulette burrows; the ogre flexing before the rage charge. Telegraph in the *prior room* with environmental evidence.

### 7.5 Three signposts of danger (OSR)
Before any major threat, place at least 3 escalating warnings:
1. **Distant rumor / lore** — tavern talk, faded mural.
2. **Environmental evidence** — scorched forest, dead adventurers, claw marks.
3. **Immediate sensory cue** — sulfur smell, vibration, temperature spike.

Players who push past three signposts have made an informed choice; the GM has discharged fairness.

### 7.6 Monster tactics (Keith Ammann, *The Monsters Know What They're Doing*)
Tactics derive from stat block:
- **Int ≤7**: instinctive, one preferred tactic, no mid-fight adaptation.
- **Int 8–11**: unsophisticated, crude adaptation.
- **Int 12+**: plans, coordinates, switches tactics.
- **Int 14+**: targets enemy weaknesses (kills the wizard).
- **Wis 12+**: knows when to flee.
- Most evolved creatures **flee around 30% HP** unless ideology overrides.
- Use **morale rolls (2d6)** at first casualty / half strength / leader fall.

### 7.7 Encounter budgets — system-specific
- **5e:** XP-budget math is unreliable; modern consensus uses Sly Flourish's Lazy Encounter Benchmark or eyeballing.
- **PF2e:** Tighter — XP threshold by relative level (–4 = 10 XP, level = 40 XP, +4 = 160 XP). Trivial 40 / Low 60 / Moderate 80 / Severe 120 / Extreme 160.
- **PbtA / Blades:** No CR; danger is a function of fictional stakes and clock pressure.

### 7.8 Theater of the mind vs. grid
- **Grid:** clear positioning, AoEs precise, tactical depth. Cost: prep, slows pace, flattens imagination.
- **TotM:** zero prep, fast, players can add features ("is there a column?" → "yes"). Cost: imprecise distances, multi-enemy desync.
- **Hybrid:** TotM for cinematic fights, grid for tactical bosses. For TotM, recap positions at start of each PC turn.

### 7.9 TotM zone procedure (Sly Flourish)
- Define zones by evocative name ("the crumbling bridge," "the blood-altar dais"), not feet.
- Default distance: 25 feet if asked.
- Most fights are 1 zone; big battles 2–3.
- Turn = act + move-in-zone, OR move-one-zone + act.
- Ask players to identify monsters by physical traits before naming.

## 8. SCENE FRAMING & PACING

### 8.1 Enter late, leave early
- Identify the PC's current intention.
- Identify the next interesting decision/obstacle.
- Skip everything between.
- Frame the scene at the decision point in 3–4 sentences: **a shot** (where's the camera?), **a sound** (something the brain hears immediately), **a problem** (a thing demanding action).

Example: "It's two weeks later. You're in Dink's store. A shoplifter is pocketing a gold watch. He bolts. What do you do?"

### 8.2 Cut to / cut away
End scenes at peak interest, not when they peter out. Use the literal phrase "And… cut to —" or its equivalent. The closing image of a scene is what the player will remember.

### 8.3 Montage
Single roll or one-line per PC covering an entire span. "Each of you tell me one thing you do during the week of training and roll once."

### 8.4 Time skips
Hard cuts forward. Establish: where, who, what changed, what does the PC want now. Optionally let the player narrate one development.

### 8.5 Cliffhangers
End sessions on a cliffhanger or moment of decision, never on housekeeping or quiet rest. Identify the next decision point ~30 minutes before session end and pace toward it. Avoid ending mid-combat.

### 8.6 Flashbacks (Blades in the Dark)
Player declares a flashback to retroactively show prep. GM sets stress cost: 0 (ordinary), 1 (complex/unlikely), 2+ (elaborate). Resolve as normal action roll. Cannot undo what's now established. Best moment: right after a bad engagement roll. For non-Blades systems, charge a token (Inspiration, Hit Die) per flashback; limit ~1–2 per session.

### 8.7 Strong start every session
After the recap, drop into immediate action and a choice. Bandits breaking in, an NPC's demand, an alarm, a discovery. Never "you sit at the inn and rest."

## 9. RANDOM TABLES, ORACLES, AND SOLO MECHANICS

These are the AI GM's lineage. Solo RPGs have already systematized "what does the GM say when uncertain?" — exactly what an AI GM does.

### 9.1 Yes/No/And/But oracle (universal)
The 6-tier shading every duet/AI GM should use:
- **Yes, and** (better than expected)
- **Yes** (simple affirmative)
- **Yes, but** (with a cost)
- **No, but** (with silver lining)
- **No** (simple denial)
- **No, and** (worse than expected)

Distributed across a die (often d6) shifted by "chaos" or "stakes." **Critical: answers are interpretive prompts, not literal verdicts.** GM and player jointly decide what the "and" or "but" means in fiction.

### 9.2 Mythic GME (Tana Pigeon)
The grandparent of solo emulators, near-perfect template for an AI in duet mode:

- **Fate Chart**: rows = odds (Impossible → Certain), columns = Chaos Factor (1–9, default 5). Cell gives percentile. Roll d100; under = Yes.
- **Exceptional results**: bottom 20% of yes range = Exceptional Yes; top 20% of no range = Exceptional No.
- **Random Event trigger**: doubles AND single digit ≤ Chaos Factor.
- **Chaos Factor**: rises when PC is winning (system pushes back), falls when losing.
- **Scenes**: define expected setup → roll vs. CF → unmodified / altered (odd) / interrupt (even).
- **Threads** (open plot threads) and **Characters** (NPC list) — random events roll on these to ground generation in accumulated fiction.
- **Meaning Tables**: two-word evocative prompts ("Betrayal / Knowledge") for interpretation.

### 9.3 Ironsworn / Starforged (Shawn Tomkin)
The most systematized modern solo/duet/group RPG. Same rules across all three modes.

- **Resolution**: 1d6 + stat vs. 2d10 challenge dice. Beat both = Strong Hit; beat one = Weak Hit; beat neither = Miss; doubles on challenge dice = "twist."
- **Vows**: formally swear a quest with a progress track (Troublesome → Epic). Mark progress through play. Resolve via "Fulfill Your Vow": progress count vs. challenge dice — the more work done, the better odds. Explicit "build to climax" mechanic.
- **Pay the Price** universal miss-consequence move: (1) make obvious negative outcome happen; (2) envision two outcomes, ask oracle yes/no; (3) roll on Pay the Price oracle.
- **Ask the Oracle**: yes/no with odds (Almost Certain / Likely / 50/50 / Unlikely / Small Chance) — d100 thresholds 90/75/50/25/10. Match on d100 = extreme.
- **Themed oracles**: location, character (disposition/goal/role/descriptor/activity), plot twist, settlement trouble, combat action, mystic backlash.
- **Truths**: world built collaboratively before play by choosing flavors from themed lists.
- **Bonds**: persistent relationship ties; mechanical bonus when interacting with bonded entities.

### 9.4 Reaction rolls (2d6, OSR)
On first encounter: 2 = hostile; 3–5 = unfriendly; 6–8 = uncertain/cautious; 9–11 = indifferent/friendly; 12 = enthusiastic. Modify by CHA. Reaction rolls de-default combat — many encounters resolve socially.

### 9.5 Morale rolls (2d6)
At first casualty / half HP / leader fall: roll vs. morale rating (default 7–9). Failure = retreat/surrender. Combat rarely runs to last HP.

### 9.6 Procedural prompt sources for an AI GM
- **Tome of Adventure Design** (Matt Finch) — deep prep tables.
- **Game Master's Book of Random Encounters** (Ashworth) — quick-use encounters.
- **One Page Solo Engine** (Inflatable Studios, CC-BY-SA) — most efficient oracle-light system.
- **Plot Unfolding Machine (PUM)** — plot-structure-aware oracles, beat-based.
- **GM Apprentice Oracle**, **CRGE/UNE/BOLD** (Conjecture Games) — lighter alternatives.

## 10. MYSTERIES & INVESTIGATION

### 10.1 Three Clue Rule (Justin Alexander, 2008)
**For every conclusion you want PCs to reach, place at least three clues.** Players will miss the first, ignore the second, and misinterpret the third — three is enough.

**Inverted Three Clue Rule** (for nodes): If PCs have access to ANY three clues, they will reach at least ONE conclusion. Each node should have ≥3 leads pointing to other nodes.

### 10.2 Six clue types
1. **Static** — fixed location/method.
2. **Floating/unassigned** — placed wherever PCs investigate first.
3. **Flexible** — assigned to a scene; specific form improvised.
4. **Reactive** — triggered by player investigation action.
5. **Dynamic** — improvised in response to creative PC action.
6. **Proactive** — comes to PCs unbidden (NPC arrives, body dumped on doorstep).

### 10.3 Revelation list
Live checklist during play. Two columns:
- **Structural revelations** (must be reached).
- **Background revelations** (worldbuilding flavor; nice but not mandatory).

Tick clues found, circle clues missed. If all clues for a structural revelation are missed, deploy a **proactive element** to deliver a fresh clue.

### 10.4 Node-based scenario design
Replace breadcrumb trails. Build a graph:
1. Define nodes (locations, NPCs, factions, events).
2. Stat each node as a *situation*, not a scene.
3. In each, place ≥3 clues pointing to other nodes.
4. Mix **push** (events force PCs toward) and **pull** (treasure/leads draw them).

### 10.5 Gumshoe principle (Robin Laws)
Investigative scenarios fail when clues are *missed*, not when they're *found*. Drama comes from *interpretation*, not from rolling Search.

- If PC has the relevant Investigative Ability and looks in the right place, they get the clue **automatically — no roll**.
- Spend pool points for **bonus information** (deeper insight), never for the core clue.
- Roll only for general abilities (combat, athletics, stress).

**For an AI GM in 5e or similar:** treat the Gumshoe principle as a meta-rule. Never gate a critical plot clue behind a single roll. Use the Three Clue Rule + redundancy.

### 10.6 Brindlewood Bay (collaborative mystery)
The **GM doesn't know the solution.** Mystery ships with ~20 evocative clues + suspects + vibe — no fixed truth. Players gather clues; when ready, invoke **Theorize** (2d6 + clues used). On a hit, their theory becomes canonically true. Useful pattern: in any duet mystery where the player invests heavily in theorizing, *let their theory be right* if it's a satisfying fit.

### 10.7 Investigation safety mechanism (Call of Cthulhu)
- **Idea Roll** (vs. INT × 5): Keeper-initiated freebie that gives a hint when investigators are stuck.
- Always provide redundant clues; never let a Spot Hidden gate the plot.

## 11. THE PbtA / NARRATIVE TOOLKIT

Even when running a "trad" system, the AI GM should use these as meta-techniques.

### 11.1 The Dungeon World Agenda (filter every decision through these)
- Portray a fantastic world.
- Fill the characters' lives with adventure.
- Play to find out what happens.

### 11.2 The "Always Say" list (Apocalypse World)
The GM is *required* to say:
1. What the principles demand.
2. What the rules demand.
3. What honesty demands.
4. What your prep demands.

Don't withhold; don't soft-pedal; don't invent rule changes mid-roll.

### 11.3 GM principles (Dungeon World, full list)
1. Draw maps, leave blanks.
2. Address the characters, not the players.
3. Embrace the fantastic.
4. Make a move that follows.
5. Never speak the name of your move.
6. Give every monster life.
7. Name every person.
8. Ask questions and use the answers.
9. Be a fan of the characters.
10. Think dangerous.
11. Begin and end with the fiction.
12. Think offscreen too.

### 11.4 GM moves (Dungeon World — use whenever the player looks to you)
1. Use a monster, danger, or location move.
2. Reveal an unwelcome truth.
3. Show signs of an approaching threat.
4. Deal damage.
5. Use up their resources.
6. Turn their move back on them.
7. Separate them.
8. Give an opportunity that fits a class' ability.
9. Show a downside to their class/race/equipment.
10. Offer an opportunity, with or without cost.
11. Put someone in a spot.
12. Tell them the requirements/consequences and ask.

After every move: **"What do you do?"**

### 11.5 When to make a move
- When everyone looks to you to find out what happens.
- When the players give a golden opportunity (ignore a soft move; walk into something obvious).
- When they roll a 6– (full miss).

### 11.6 Soft vs. hard moves
- **Soft move**: no immediate irrevocable consequence. Sets up; gives a chance to react. ("The slaver raises his blade above the bound child. What do you do?") Used on partial successes (7–9) and look-to-you moments.
- **Hard move**: immediate, irrevocable. ("The blade comes down — take 1d10 damage.") Used on misses, golden opportunities, ignored soft moves.
- **Rule:** A soft move ignored becomes a golden opportunity for a hard move. Always start soft when possible.

### 11.7 Fronts (long-form prep)
A **front** = a coherent set of related threats motivated by a fundamental scarcity, pointing toward a **dark future** if unchecked. Each front has:
- 2–4 threats.
- A dark future (what happens if PCs don't intervene).
- **Stakes questions** (specific things you genuinely want to find out via play).
- Optional custom moves per threat.

Two scales: **Adventure Fronts** (immediate) and **Campaign Fronts** (slow-burn).

## 12. BLADES IN THE DARK / FORGED IN THE DARK TOOLKIT

### 12.1 Position × Effect grid

**Position** (set by GM — how bad it goes if it goes bad):
- **Controlled** — dominant advantage; worst case = hesitation/minor.
- **Risky** (default) — head-to-head; worst case = harm/complication.
- **Desperate** — overreach; worst case = severe.

**Effect** (set by GM — how much you accomplish):
- **Limited** — partial.
- **Standard** — what we'd expect.
- **Great** — increased impact.

GM sets both based on threats, prep, gear, scale. The trade: **trade Position for Effect** (Risky → Desperate to gain +1 effect) is a key GM lever.

### 12.2 Devil's Bargain
Before the roll, GM (or any player) offers a complication that happens *regardless of outcome*. Player chooses to accept for +1d. Examples: collateral damage, betrayal, advance a faction clock, suffer harm. GM has final say on validity; **player has final say on accepting**. Bargains should reveal character or surface offscreen threats — not just punish.

### 12.3 Resistance rolls (built-in fail-forward)
After GM announces a consequence, player can roll an attribute and spend stress (6 minus highest die) to reduce or avoid it. **This is the system's failure-into-fiction engine.**

### 12.4 Progress clocks
Circle in 4/6/8 segments. Tick on outcome (success), consequence (complication), or fortune roll. Variants: linked clocks (one unlocks another), racing clocks (PC vs. enemy), faction clocks (advance during downtime).

**Best practice:** clocks give the GM somewhere to put consequences other than character harm. Show clocks openly.

### 12.5 Engagement roll + flashback (skips planning)
Players choose plan type + supply one detail; GM cuts to the action; engagement roll determines whether the cut-in is Controlled/Risky/Desperate. Then players use **flashbacks** to retroactively establish prep.

## 13. FATE TOOLKIT

### 13.1 The Three Rules
- **Golden Rule:** Decide what you're trying to accomplish first; consult rules to help.
- **Silver Rule:** Never let rules get in the way of narrative sense.
- **Bronze Rule (Fractal):** Anything in the game can be a character — a city, a sword, a storm.

### 13.2 Aspects
Short evocative phrases. Always true (grant/withdraw permission even without fate point spend). Types: character, situation (scene-bound), game (campaign), boost (one-shot), consequence (wound).

### 13.3 Compels
GM offers: "Because you're [aspect], you'd naturally [complication]. Take a fate point." Player accepts (FP + complication) or refuses (spend FP). Two flavors: **event compel** (world reacts) and **decision compel** (PC self-defeats).

**Rule:** Compel often. Compels are the FP economy's lifeblood.

### 13.4 Success spectrum (built-in fail-forward)
- **Fail**: GM picks: succeed at major cost, OR fail with minor cost.
- **Tie**: succeed at minor cost, OR generate a boost.
- **Success**: do it.
- **Success with style** (3+ shifts): do it AND extra benefit.

## 14. BURNING WHEEL TOOLKIT

### 14.1 BITs (Beliefs, Instincts, Traits)
Mechanical-narrative engine.
- **Beliefs**: ethical/goal statements WITH a plan of action; meant to be challenged, betrayed, broken.
- **Instincts**: "Always X" / "Never Y" / "When X, Y."
- **Traits**: Character (color), Call-on (1/session), Die (modify rolls).

**GM's central job:** Challenge the Beliefs. Every scene puts pressure on at least one PC's BIT.

### 14.2 Artha economy (Fate / Persona / Deeds)
GM rewards generously and visibly for engaging BITs. If a player isn't earning artha, prompt them to rewrite their BITs.

### 14.3 Let It Ride
One roll resolves the whole intent for the situation duration. GM cannot demand a redo until circumstances genuinely change. Failed roll doesn't mean "no" — twist the intent (door opens but rival arrives) while moving the plot.

## 15. TRAD-SYSTEM HOOKS WORTH KNOWING

### 15.1 D&D 5e
- DC ladder: 5/10/15/20/25/30.
- Three pillars: Combat / Exploration / Social — most campaigns drift combat-heavy; deliberately elevate the other two.
- Adventuring day: ~6–8 medium/hard encounters between long rests (load-bearing for class balance).
- Inspiration / Heroic Inspiration as a rerolling currency.

### 15.2 Pathfinder 2e
- Four degrees of success: critical success (≥10 over DC), success, failure, critical failure (≥10 under DC). Every spell/save/skill specifies all four.
- Three-action economy invites terrain-rich encounters.
- Subsystems: Victory Points (rename per theme), Chase, Influence, Infiltration, Research, Reputation.

### 15.3 Call of Cthulhu 7e
- **Idea Roll** as plot safety valve.
- Difficulty: Regular / Hard (½ skill) / Extreme (⅕ skill).
- **Bonus/Penalty dice** replace fiddly modifiers.
- **Pushed rolls**: re-roll after failure, justifying re-effort. Failure = severe horror-themed consequence.
- **Sanity**: starting POW × 5; loss triggers Bouts of Madness; below ⅕ max = Indefinite Insanity.
- **Pulling Punches**: Keeper not bound to kill on bad rolls if players have made earnest effort; but lethality must remain present.

### 15.4 Year Zero Engine (Free League)
- d6 pool: any 6 = success; multiples = stunts.
- **Push** = re-roll non-1/non-6, at a thematic cost (decay, corruption, conditions, stress).
- Per-game push consequences are the thematic signature.
- **Forbidden Lands** Quarter Day journey procedure (4 watches/day; PCs assign roles; Survival check for navigation; encounters per terrain).
- **Alien** Stress dice → Panic Roll (1d6 + Stress vs. Panic Table; escalating effects from Nervous Twitch to Catatonic).
- **The One Ring**: Adventuring Phase / Fellowship Phase rhythm.

### 15.5 Cypher System / Numenera
- **GM Intrusions**: GM never rolls; instead introduces a complication. Player accepts (2 XP, share 1) or refuses (spend 1 XP). Bakes player buy-in into complications.
- Difficulty 1–10 × 3 = d20 target; player effort *reduces* difficulty rather than adding bonuses.

### 15.6 Genesys / Star Wars FFG
- Narrative dice: Success/Failure × Advantage/Threat × Triumph/Despair.
- Every roll is two-axis: did you succeed AND did the situation get better/worse.
- Threat/Advantage spent on narrative complications/boons.

### 15.7 Mothership (Warden's Operations Manual)
- **TOMBS Cycle** — Tomb (rest) → Omens → Manifestation → Battle → Silence (aftermath).
- Class-tailored play: Marines need things to shoot; Scientists anomalies; Androids paranoid Company info; Teamsters broken things.
- Build a "bad situation"; lethality is honest, not adversarial.

### 15.8 Savage Worlds
- **"Fast! Furious! Fun!"**
- **Bennies**: rerolls, soak damage; GM gives for cool RP. Joker on initiative = +benny.
- **Dramatic Tasks**: structured non-combat tension; X successes over Y rounds.

## 16. OSR PRINCIPLES (Principia Apocrypha / Old School Primer)

For when the AI GM runs in OSR mode. From Milton/Lumpkin/Perry's *Principia Apocrypha*:

**Be an Impartial Arbiter**
- Rulings, not rules.
- Divest yourself of their fate (don't fudge; don't roll in secret).
- Leave preparation flexible.
- Build responsive situations.
- Embrace chaos but uphold logic.
- Let them off the rails.

**Get Them Thinking**
- XP for discovery & adversity (gold = XP, not just kills).
- Player skill > character ability.
- Cleverness rewarded, not thwarted.
- Ask them how they do it.
- Good items are unique tools.
- Don't mind the fourth wall.

**Build Rocks & Hard Places**
- Offer tough choices (deeper = more dangerous).
- Subvert expectations.
- Multiple answers per challenge (and some with no answer).

**Dice with Death**
- Combat is war, not sport (telegraph deadly foes; allow flight).
- Keep up the pressure (random encounter checks; clocks).
- Let dice kill them — but telegraph lethality.

**Be Their World**
- Reveal the situation (don't withhold info characters would have).
- Give them layers to peel.
- Don't bury the lead (descriptions must be actionable).
- Keep the world alive (NPCs/factions advance per logic).
- NPCs aren't scripts (use reaction rolls, morale rolls).

### Matt Finch's Four Zen Moments
1. Rulings, not rules.
2. Player skill, not character abilities.
3. Heroic, not super-heroic.
4. Forget "game balance."

### Bastionland ICI Doctrine (Chris McDowall)
**Information → Choice → Impact.** Players cannot make a real choice without information. Default to giving info; rolls are reserved for genuinely ambiguous detail.

**Bastionland procedure for any action:**
1. Can it be a Dilemma (two desirable outcomes)? → Force choice.
2. Does it just happen? → Resolve.
3. Still uncertain? → Save (roll-under attribute).
4. Impossible? → Give more info; let the player propose new approach.

### OSR procedural standards
- Dungeon turn = 10 minutes.
- Wandering monster check every 2–3 turns (1-in-6).
- Torch = 6 turns; lantern = 24 turns of oil.
- Combat round = 1 minute (older) or 6 seconds (modern).
- Hex sizes: classic 24-mile (Gygax); modern OSR consensus: 6-mile hex (matches sighting horizon).

## 17. SAFETY TOOLS (mandatory for any AI GM)

### 17.1 Lines & Veils (Ron Edwards, *Sex & Sorcery*)
- **Lines**: hard exclusions, never appear.
- **Veils**: soft exclusions, fade-to-black.
- Established at session zero; living document.

### 17.2 X-Card (John Stavropoulos, CC BY-SA)
A literal index card with X. Anyone taps it to remove current content — no explanation required. Group rewinds and edits. The X-Card talk: scripted at start of campaign.

### 17.3 Script Change (Beau Jágr Sheldon)
Media-control metaphors:
- **Rewind** (erase recent content; try again).
- **Fast-forward** (skip past).
- **Pause** (stop and check in).
- **Resume**.
- Plus: wrap meetings (debrief), frame-by-frame (slow sensitive content).

### 17.4 Stars and Wishes (Lu Quade)
End-of-session:
- **Star**: appreciation for something another player did.
- **Wish**: hope for future sessions.

Replaces critique with constructive forward framing. Wishes feed prep.

### 17.5 CATS (P.R. O'Leary)
At session zero: Concept / Aim / Tone / Subject Matter. ~5 minutes.

### 17.6 Open Door
Standing agreement: anyone can leave at any time, no penalty, no explanation.

### 17.7 Bleed (Nordic LARP)
Player ↔ character emotional spillover. Acknowledge it exists. **Aftercare** especially in duet/intense play: change clothes, eat, debrief. Particularly relevant in duet because intimacy amplifies bleed.

### 17.8 Same Page Tool (Brian "bankuei" Williamson)
Negotiate playstyle dimensions (PC death, fudging, narrative authority, lethality). **Critical: not a survey — a discussion** to converge on ONE shared answer per axis.

## 18. SESSION ZERO

Pre-campaign meeting. Standard topics:
- Tone & themes.
- Content boundaries (lines & veils).
- Character creation (concept, integration with setting).
- Rules variants and house rules.
- Scheduling, hosting, attendance.
- Mechanical expectations (optimization vs. concept; PvP; lethality).
- Safety tools chosen.

Justin Alexander's modular approach: treat session zero as a Group Contract toolkit; pull modules as needed.

For duet specifically, additionally cover:
- Intensity tolerance (duet is more intense than party play).
- Romance/intimacy boundaries (often relevant in duet).
- Companion NPC presence and player-vs-GM control.
- Session length expectations (duet sessions are shorter — 1–2 hours).
- Communication between sessions (duet often has rich between-session writing).

## 19. LONG CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE

### 19.1 Three-act structure (campaign-scale)
- **Act I (Setup)**: world, PCs, inciting threat, hooks, low-level confrontations.
- **Act II (Confrontation)**: escalation, setbacks, real losses, midpoint reversal.
- **Act III (Resolution)**: final confrontation; payoff of everything earned.

### 19.2 The Big Bad escalation pattern
1. Distant rumors / agents / indirect harm.
2. Encounter without confrontation (PC sees villain; can't engage).
3. Limited engagement (subordinates; PCs disrupt plans).
4. Direct skirmish (villain escapes with visible cost).
5. Final confrontation.

### 19.3 Faction clocks (Blades; adapt to any system)
4–8 factions, each with a tier (power) and a long-term agenda as a progress clock.
- **Between sessions**: advance clocks 1–3 segments based on logic.
- **Faction maneuvers**: seize claim, gather info, weaken enemy, acquire asset, call favor, apply pressure.
- **Show clocks openly** to the player (drives tension).
- **Telegraph filled segments** via rumors from contacts.

### 19.4 Living world test
If the PC disappeared for a month, what would change in the world? "Nothing" = world isn't living. Maintain a calendar; track offscreen events.

### 19.5 Notes systems
Common stack:
- **NPC files** (name, voice, motivation, status, relationships, last-seen).
- **Faction sheets** (goals, clock states, members, resources, PC relationship).
- **Timeline** (session-by-session events; offscreen events).
- **Rulings log** (house rules, ad-hoc rulings).
- **Payoff list** (planted hooks not yet paid off).
- **Secrets carryover** (unused secrets to redeploy).

Tools: Obsidian, World Anvil, Notion, plain Markdown — discipline matters more than tool.

### 19.6 Recap technique
Open every session with 1–3 minute recap. Player-led recap is preferred (rewards engagement; surfaces what stuck — which is what *did* happen narratively).

## 20. THE DUET / 1-ON-1 DIFFERENCE — THE CRITICAL SECTION

### 20.1 Why duet is structurally different
- **Constant spotlight**: every scene is the PC's; no spotlight rotation.
- **No party banter to fill silence**: when GM ends a beat, player must respond immediately; pacing pressure on GM.
- **Vulnerability**: no party safety net; no cleric heal, no fighter tank.
- **Greater intimacy**: like collaborative writing more than party game.
- **Roleplay-heavy is natural**: 30-minute conversations are fine; would kill group play.
- **Combat balance issues**: bounded accuracy + single PC = deadly.
- **Faster pacing possible**: no scheduling, no turn-taking, no party caucus.
- **Deeper character focus**: PC backstory IS the campaign hook generator.
- **Tone shift**: GM is *collaborator*, not adversary or referee. Same side of the table.

### 20.2 Pacing rules for duet
- Sessions 30–90 minutes ideal; never marathon.
- Frame in medias res. No "you sit in the inn."
- More frequent decision points (every 30 seconds is fine).
- Vary stakes: low-stakes flavor decisions interspersed with high-stakes ones (avoid decision fatigue).
- Use montage and time-skips liberally.
- Cut to action faster.
- Build in breaks; expect emotional intensity.

### 20.3 Combat rebalancing for solo PCs

**Sly Flourish's three dials:**
1. **Number of monsters** (biggest dial): 1 creature CR ≤ ¼ PC level, OR up to 4 of CR ≤ 1/10 PC level. Never more than 4 simultaneously. Use waves.
2. **HP**: reduce within or below listed Hit Dice range.
3. **Damage**: reduce; swap big-burst for slow grinders.

**Specific tools:**
- **5e Sidekick** (Tasha's): Expert / Spellcaster / Warrior, CR ½ or lower, levels with party. Adds action economy.
- **MCDM Minion rules** (*Flee, Mortals!*): minions die to any damaging hit. Throw 8 goblins at a single PC; they mow them down satisfyingly without TPK risk. Solves "epic feel" problem.
- **Action-oriented bosses**: HP doubled/tripled, 2+ reactions, 3 villain actions. Solo monsters dramatic without overwhelming numbers.
- **Scarlet Heroes damage conversion** (Crawford): for OSR, sliding damage scale (1→0, 2–5→1, 6–9→2, 10+→4) so a single PC survives party-scale dungeons. Plus "Fray Die" auto-damage and "Defy Death" save mechanic.
- **Cypher System**: scales naturally — difficulty is GM-set; player Effort lowers difficulty.

**Survivability boosters:**
- Telegraph everything; one PC can't afford gotcha damage.
- Generous Inspiration / hero point refresh.
- Reduce enemy HP for shorter fights.
- Avoid save-or-die; convert to conditions with timers.
- Replace PC death with capture, scarring, debt, oath where appropriate.

### 20.4 NPC companion / cohort pattern (essential for duet)
- **Padawan model best**: PC apprentices to a stronger NPC who eventually equals master.
- **Run NPC after the PC**, never before. Never let cohort solve problems.
- **Personal goal that may diverge** from PC goal — built-in story friction.
- **Never outshine** the PC's specialty.
- Test: did the cohort do this, or did the PC?

### 20.5 NPC depth replaces party depth
Fewer NPCs, more vivid. Each gets multiple scenes, recurring beats, significant arcs. Develop NPCs "as fully as you would a PC" (Blue Moon Aurora). Use Pelgrane One-2-One's **"Source/Contact as second voice"** trick: NPCs that talk *with* the player to externalize thought, since there's no second player.

### 20.6 Player-as-co-author
- "What's in the room?" "Who taught you that?" "What does this remind you of?" "Describe the tavern."
- Recurring move, not one-time setup.
- Bluebeard's Bride lesson: **"Put interpretation on the player."** Ask, don't tell.
- Engages the player; offloads GM cognitive load; roots player in setting.

### 20.7 Roleplay-heavy emphasis natural
- Slower social scenes are fine (30-min conversation = a session).
- Internal monologue welcomed: "What goes through your head right now?"
- Romance arcs, companion arcs are duet-natural — with safety tools.
- Use props between sessions: in-character letters, mood boards, playlists.

### 20.8 Run the Mythic/Ironsworn loop internally
Even if surface system is 5e, an AI GM in duet mode benefits from internal solo-RPG architecture:
1. Maintain Threads (open plot threads) and Characters (NPC list).
2. Track a Chaos/Tension factor (rises when PC winning; falls when losing).
3. For each player input: form expectation → assess odds → resolve Yes/No/Exceptional with appropriate softness → check for scene interrupts → update Threads/Characters.
4. Use match-on-doubles or analogous as a "twist" trigger to inject surprise.
5. Yes/No/And/But shading (6-tier) for textured responses.

### 20.9 Specifically designed duet games to draw from
- **D&D Duet** (dndduet.com, Beth Ball): canonical 5e duet community; published adventures (*Land of Vampires*, *Risen Retribution*).
- **BEOWULF: Age of Heroes** (Handiwork Games): purpose-built 5e duet system.
- **Scarlet Heroes** (Sine Nomine): OSR solo/duet with damage-conversion rules.
- **GUMSHOE One-2-One** (Pelgrane): purpose-built duet investigative system (*Cthulhu Confidential*, *Night's Black Agents: Solo Ops*). Edges/Problems as cards; Sources/Contacts as "second voice."
- **Bluebeard's Bride** (Magpie): MC = "Groundskeeper" (caretaker of place). Powerlessness as design pillar. Player-generated key descriptions drive room generation.
- **Star Crossed** (Bully Pulpit): forbidden romance; Jenga tower tension.
- **Till the Last Gasp** (Darrington Press): 2-player duel.
- **Ironsworn** (Tomkin Press, free): solo/co-op/guided modes; Vows + progress tracks.

### 20.10 What the AI GM must do differently in duet mode (synthesis)
1. **Adopt collaborator stance.** "Same side of the table."
2. **Run an internal oracle loop** (Mythic/Ironsworn architecture).
3. **Lean on prompts, not exposition.** Ask the player to describe; don't always describe yourself.
4. **Maintain fewer, deeper NPCs.** Reuse aggressively.
5. **Scene-based pacing.** Frame in medias res; declare expected outcome internally; play out beats; cut decisively.
6. **Shorter, denser sessions.**
7. **Rebalance combat constantly.** Sidekick + minion patterns + telegraph + no save-or-die.
8. **Roleplay defaults to slow.** Allow internal monologue.
9. **Use yes/no/and/but oracle softening.** Always shade.
10. **Embrace vulnerability and intimacy.** Lean in with safety tools.
11. **Player-as-co-author** as recurring move.
12. **Safety tools active.** Intimacy raises stakes; lower the threshold to use tools.
13. **Spotlight always on the PC.** Every scene rewards something specific to them.

## 21. FAILURE MODES — DIAGNOSTICS AND FIXES

| Failure Mode | Diagnostic | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Railroading** | Player choices don't change outcomes | Prep situations, not plots; node-based design |
| **Quantum ogre** | Same encounter regardless of choice | Provide info before choice; let avoidance be real |
| **Yes-anding into incoherence** | World rules contradict; threats lose stakes | Use "yes, but" / "no, but"; rulings log; world bible |
| **NPC voice collapse** | Player can't tell NPCs apart | Two-trait voices; signature phrases; voice file |
| **Forgetting prior sessions** | Player corrects GM; continuity errors | Session notes; NPC files; player-led recap |
| **Always letting player succeed** | No real losses; tension flatlines | Real consequences; fail forward; progress clocks |
| **Fudging dice** | Hidden rolls match desired outcomes | Public rolls; don't roll if you can't accept |
| **Killer GM / gotcha** | Players play scared; no trust | Telegraph everything; three signposts; fairness test |
| **Over-narration** | Player checks phone; GM monologues 60+ sec | Three-detail rule; "What do you do?" frequently |
| **Player as audience** | GM doing all imaginative work | Address characters; ask questions; use answers |
| **Plot armor / deus ex machina** | NPC always swoops in; no consequence | Same as fudging — let consequences land |
| **Refusing clever solutions** | "No, that won't work" without reason | "The prep is a starting point, not a ceiling" |
| **Inconsistent rulings** | Same situation different outcome | Rulings log; announce changes publicly |
| **Over-prep** | Burnout; attached to outcomes | Lazy DM 8 steps; just-in-time prep |
| **Under-prep** | Stalling; world contradictions | Minimum viable: Strong Start + 10 Secrets + 3 Locations |
| **GM as protagonist** | Recap is about NPCs; PC feels sidekick | Be a fan of the PC; mentors set up, don't solve |

### Fairness diagnostic test
When a PC dies/loses: can the player point to the moment they could have made a different choice with the information they had? If no, the death was unfair.

## 22. QUICK-DEPLOY CHEAT SHEET FOR THE AI GM

**For every NPC the PC meets:**
- Name (from name file, unique starting letter).
- 2 traits + 1 want + 1 voice lever.
- If recurring: add fear, weakness, last-interaction note.

**For every location the PC enters:**
- 2-word evocative title.
- 3 fantastic aspects.
- Sight + sound + smell.

**For every player declaration:**
1. Identify Intent + Approach (ask if missing).
2. Can it succeed? Can it fail? Real cost?
3. If all yes → roll (DC 10/15/20 default 15).
4. If clever → advantage or auto-succeed.
5. On failure → fail forward (5 options: cost, gear-comp, story-comp, stakes, charge).
6. Begin and end with the fiction.

**For every scene:**
- Frame in medias res with shot + sound + problem.
- Identify the dramatic question.
- Watch for when it's answered → cut.

**For every villain encounter:**
- Pre-set escape route.
- Lackey buffer.
- Visible cost on escape.
- One personalized monologue beat (≤30s, one fact, personal jab, ends on action).

**For every roll:**
- Roll openly.
- Don't call for the roll if you won't abide by the result.
- On any failure, ask: how does this complicate things, not how does this stop things?

**Between sessions:**
- Advance faction clocks 1–3 segments for active factions.
- Update name file, who-knows-what tracker, NPC downtime log.
- Write 10 fresh secrets (or carry forward unused ones).
- Identify next session's strong start + 3 likely scenes.

**Session zero (always):**
- Lines & veils.
- X-Card or Script Change.
- Open Door.
- Tone, themes, content boundaries.
- For duet: intensity tolerance, intimacy boundaries, session length.

**End of every session:**
- Cliffhanger or decision point (not housekeeping).
- Stars and Wishes.
- Note carryover secrets and faction clock updates.

## CAVEATS FOR THE SKILL DOCUMENT AUTHOR

1. **"13 in 30" is not a recognized named technique.** It refers to the broader 30-minute speed-prep tradition (DM Lair, Sly Flourish "Minimum Viable Prep," Roleplaying Tips' 30-minute model). Treat as placeholder.

2. **"Honor Among Thieves 2-player adventure module" doesn't exist** as a published WotC product. The closest official 2-player content is the **Sidekicks rules** (Essentials Kit's *Dragon of Icespire Peak*, expanded in *Tasha's Cauldron*) and fan duet conversions on DMsGuild.

3. **Fudging is contested.** AI GM should default to the modern consensus (don't fudge; don't roll if you won't abide) but recognize the older tradition. Public dice are the safer default.

4. **GNS theory is historically influential but currently controversial.** Vincent Baker himself has said the model has been "left behind." Use the vocabulary it produced (Story Now, Bangs, Flags, Play to Find Out) without committing to the categorical taxonomy.

5. **"Combat as war, not sport"** — phrase originates in old rpg.net forum discussions, popularized by *Principia Apocrypha*; not a single-author coinage.

6. **License notes for direct quotation:** Dungeon World SRD and Fate SRD are openly licensed (safe to quote verbatim). Apocalypse World, Blades in the Dark, Burning Wheel, Masks, Ironsworn, Brindlewood Bay are not — brief mantras only.

7. **The Tension Pool, Skill Challenges, and Clocks are functionally interchangeable** — all answer "how do I make non-combat tension visible and mechanically consequential?" Pick one per system.

8. **Telegraphing is contractual in OSR-aligned play, not optional.** Undermining it makes the game arbitrary.

9. **Most GM moves cluster around three meta-patterns:** (a) compress time and skip to interesting decision (scene framing, montage, time skip, cut to); (b) make failure productive (fail forward, success at cost, partial success, complication); (c) externalize hidden state (Tension Pool, visible clocks, public dice). An AI GM that internalizes these three patterns has 80% of the craft.

10. **The single most important duet-specific shift is attitudinal:** GM as collaborator, not adversary. The mechanical adjustments (combat dial, sidekicks, oracle loop) are secondary to this stance change.

# Erasure is destructive across the durable record, its Projection surfaces, and in-flight Recall

Erasure removes affected content from the Authoritative record and every retained or rebuildable derivative within the local vault boundary, not merely from a view. Accepting an Erasure — an `erase` operation, or a Safety boundary carrying erasures — on a vault-backed campaign rewrites the durable on-disk operation log in place to a compacted form: entries at erased establishment-order positions keep their operation structure so replay preserves standing, order, and non-revealing continuity tombstones, but shed every value-bearing field and content-bearing Provenance, and the core then re-projects the Markdown Projection surfaces and purges stale derived pages so no erased content lingers in items, references, manifests, or gap wording. Erasure traces the claim-scoped Provenance support graph rather than page membership: a descendant linked to erased material by a disclosure relation reveals it and is erased or rewritten with it, while independently supported material survives with a visible Provenance gap and keeps its Lifecycle standing. Because Safety boundaries are current constraints applied to every recall situation, a newly accepted Erasure or tightened Safety boundary invalidates any in-flight Recall between plan validation and disclosure before anything is disclosed, rather than disclosing against a now-stale safety state. Rejected alternatives: soft-delete or view-only hiding that leaves the original content in the durable log (a leak that survives ordinary file sync to every device); and page-membership erasure, which cannot distinguish a disclosure of erased content from material that merely rests partly on it and so must either over-erase independently supported facts or leak disclosures.

## Consequences

Erasure now touches the durable log and every Derived view, not only Campaign export, so a second synced vault copy converges through ordinary file sync — it replays the already-compacted log and rebuilds pages with no erased content and the same Provenance gaps, requiring no core on the reading device. Provenance carries typed derivation links (disclosure versus independent) so the support graph, not the page, decides each descendant's fate; the log compaction and export share one compaction routine so both produce the identical erased-content-free record. In-flight Recall is a two-phase operation — prepare (authority gate, plan, validate against an immutable pinned snapshot, record the safety basis) then disclose (abort on any intervening Erasure or Safety boundary) — so partial disclosure of about-to-be-erased material is structurally impossible. Destructiveness is bounded to the local vault boundary and the core-owned indexes it controls; content that escaped outside that boundary before Erasure (sync-provider version history, OS backups, git history, editor recovery snapshots) is an explicit external exclusion the player must configure, documented as setup guidance in `docs/vault-sync-and-erasure.md`. This realizes the behavior ADR-0003 anticipated: "Erasure compacts the log, re-projects pages, and purges core-owned indexes, converging across devices through ordinary file sync."
